# Mercury: the Hemerodromoi at the endgame

> Kalliope, 2026-10-20. Written to `endgame/CONTRACT.md`. Sources: LORE.md (integration-3), FEUDS.md, ENDGAME.md,
> GOD-ARMOUR.md, and Caleb's ruling of 2026-10-20 that the game's year is 6000 and the Founding was around 3000.
> Original writing; no published game text or SRD material is used. **Numbers: FIRST PASS, for Palamedes review.**
> They all live in section I. Effects not on the contract's list are under "New effects needed" at the end.

## A. The house at the endgame

By the late game Mercury has the smallest home fleet and the most ships in the field, because half of it is on someone
else's payroll. At home: one Hoplon, one destroyer, two Embolon, four Aspis and a handful of Skytale runners. Everything
else is out as hired companies. Mercury fears the dawn and the stop. In the war it fears one more thing: the end of it.
A system with one ruler has nobody left to bid. What Mercury wants from the war is that it goes on.

**Hiring and the outbid (the rule every Mercury card uses).**
- A company is a **lochos** (one Embolon, two Aspis) or a **mora** (one destroyer, two Embolon, four Aspis). Any house
  Wary or better with Mercury can hire one for one term. Mercury is never in a blood feud, so every house can bid.
- Mercury never breaks a contract. It lets one run out. When a term ends the company is up for renewal, and anyone can
  bid on it, including the house it is shooting at.
- If another bid beats your renewal by the outbid margin, the company signs the new contract where it stands and turns
  its guns on you. **The tell:** a Skytale crosses the field to the company's Embolon one warning period before the
  term ends. Everyone sees it land.
- A **sealed contract** (both halves of the staff, carried by Skytale) costs more and skips renewal for two terms.
  Nobody can buy it out while it runs.
- A company under contract keeps it against other Hemerodromoi, and keeps it at Lakedaimon.

**At the Reckoning (3 worlds).** Mercury does not drift Hostile. It gets offers. Every house that drifts toward Hostile
with you starts bidding for companies to send against you. Mercury's standing with you falls only if you let a
contract lapse unpaid or fire on a company you hired.

**In the Last Alliance (5 worlds).** Mercury joins only when paid. The alliance pools one bid for its companies.
Outbid the pool and Mercury stays home, or flies for you. It is the one member of the alliance you can buy out of it.

**As your ally at the betrayal.** Mercury opens no gates out of anger. The alliance bids for the gates, and the company
on your station waits for its term to end (card MER-E8). Unless your standing is at maximum, you hold Lakedaimon in
chains, or you match the bid, the doors open. A loyal Mercury has no blood enemy to name, so its price is a world: the
one of yours that makes the most money.

**Playing as Mercury.** No blood enemy, contracts on every side: the hard mode. You can hire your own companies out to
AI houses for credits. Every company you rent out is one not guarding Lakedaimon, and it keeps the contract even if
its client later turns on you.

## B. The caste

**Where the edit came from.** Lakedaimon's rail is one loop round the planet's equator, about 15,330 km. At any hour
most of it lies in the full day at 590–725 K, where it warps, and the city meets that rail again when the night brings
it round. In the founding year of the walking city, the first King of the Rail put the question to the first ephors:
send citizens into the day and lose them, or make people who could stand it. The ephors voted for the second. They
edited the children of the rail crews, and only those children. The citizens' glare edit (dark skin, the second clear
eyelid) was given to everyone. The heat edit was given to the crews and written into the city's law as property of
the Rail. That vote is a long way back now, nearly as far as the Founding, and the helots have been laying rail every
day of it.

**What the edit does.** Helots run hot. Their bodies hold a core temperature that would kill a citizen, and their
cells repair radiation damage during sleep. A helot suit weighs half a citizen's suit because the body inside does
the rest of the work. The second eyelid is thicker and stays shut on the day side; they work by touch and by the
suit's display. They are shorter than citizens and broad in the shoulder, built for lifting rail, not for the long
walk.

**A day of work.** A shift starts at the tail of the city, nearest the dawn. The forward gang rides a crawler ten hours
west into the dark, to where the last day bent the rail. They find the bent lengths by lamplight, cut them out, and lay
new ones before the city's front wheels arrive. The day gang goes the other way, back into the sun, to the Beam Towers
and the stretch of rail that will come round next. Nobody rides back. The gangs wait at the rail for the city to reach
them, and climb on at walking pace.

**What they call themselves.** The city's word is *Helots*, the old Spartan word for the state's own bonded workers,
and the whole system uses it now as slang. The caste calls itself the *Hemerobatai*, the "day-walkers". The citizens are
the day-runners. The helots are the ones who walk back into it.

**Their saying.** "They run from the day. We walk into it and bring back the rail."

**What they want.** A share of the power they make, because every beam sold to Venus crosses rail they laid. And
children born without the heat edit, who can choose the day or not.

**Two leaders.**
- **Aristomene**, foreman of the forward gang. *Wants:* the rail to belong to the people who lay it. *Lie:* she tells
  the ephors the forward rail is sound. Nine kilometres of it are not, and she left them that way on purpose (MER-C1).
- **Kinadon**, boss of the day gang at the Beam Towers. *Wants:* his daughter, who is eight, never edited. *Lie:* he
  tells his crews the King of the Rail has promised an end to the edit at the next Mercury year. No king has promised
  anything. He believes saying it will make one.

## C. The breaking point

LORE.md: "A section of the rails breaks ahead of Lakedaimon; there are 300 hours to fix it before the dawn arrives."
The chain plays from the chair when you hold Lakedaimon, and from outside when you attack it. Choice 1 of MER-C1 shows
only when you are attacking; choice 2 only when you hold the city.

### MER-C1: Nine Kilometres
- Trigger: worlds held 4+, and either (a) you hold Lakedaimon in chains and its World 4 unrest roll fires with the
  garrison below need, or (b) you are Hostile with Mercury and Lakedaimon is on your target list
- Read-aloud: A helot foreman waits at the tail of the city with her mask off. She says nine kilometres of forward rail
  will fail if someone asks it to. She has a price. She will take it from whoever pays first.
- Choices:
  1. Buy the break (attacking): credits(-150), flag(rail-cut), card(MER-C2)
  2. Buy the location and fix it quietly (holding): credits(-150), garrison(mercury-capital, +1), flag(rail-sound)
  3. Send her away: flag(aristomene-sells), card(MER-C2). She sells it to the next bidder, and it breaks anyway.

### MER-C2: The Rail Stops
- Trigger: flag(rail-cut) or flag(aristomene-sells), while you hold Lakedaimon
- Read-aloud: Lakedaimon stops. The wheels go quiet for the first time in living memory. The dawn is 300 hours behind
  you and closing at walking pace. The helots stand by the broken rail and wait to hear what you will offer.
- Choices:
  1. Drive them back to the rail: chainWorld(mercury-capital), unrest(mercury-capital, +25), troops(-10), card(MER-C3)
  2. Break the chains and ask: breakWorld(mercury-capital), income(mercury-capital, -30%), card(MER-C3)
  3. Ignore it: unrest(mercury-capital, +40), garrison(mercury-capital, -2), card(MER-C3). The ephors give the order
     without you, and they give it with whips.

### MER-C3: Three Hundred Hours
- Trigger: MER-C2 resolved
- Read-aloud: New rail goes down by lamplight, one length at a time. A hundred hours are left. The tail of the city is
  warm to the touch. Aristomene asks you one question, and she asks it once: does it walk again.
- Choices:
  1. Rebuild with free hands (broken only): credits(-200), troops(+12), pilots(+3), ships(messene, +1),
     standing(venus, -15), standing(earth, -10), flag(lakedaimon-free)
  2. Rebuild with the whip (chains only): unrest(mercury-capital, +15), troops(-5), flag(lakedaimon-chained)
  3. Let it burn: income(mercury-capital, -100%), troops(+30), standing(venus, -30), standing(mercury, -40),
     flag(lakedaimon-burned)

**When you lit the fuse from outside.** If you took MER-C1 choice 1, the AI holder plays C2 and C3 without you. Mercury
loses garrison(mercury-capital, -2) while the city stands still, and if you reach the Katabasis before the rail is
rebuilt, the drop lands on a stopped city: the heat clock in F starts at half.

## D. Held and broken

**Held in chains.** Lakedaimon pays its base income plus beam sales, most of them to Venus. It needs a garrison of 3
and starts at unrest 10. It rolls the World 4 unrest event like every chained world. Holding Lakedaimon makes you the
contract office: companies still in the field finish their terms, then come to you for the next one. You can hire
Mercury companies at a discount, and you can rent them out to the AI houses for credits each term.

**Broken.** You get troops, pilots and one **Messene**, and income drops while the rail is rebuilt, then settles above
the chained rate, because free crews fix track faster than driven ones. Garrison need falls to 1. Companies keep their
contracts, but a company you rent out from a broken Lakedaimon cannot be outbid by a house that keeps helots: free
crews refuse that money. *(Invented here; Palamedes and the AI thread to check it can be read.)*

**The Messene** (`mercury-messene`, new): a Beam Tower tug with its mirror fan turned outward, a black iron box under a
spread of gold-rimmed mirrors that throws focused sunlight at close range. Helots fly it in half-weight suits.

**What it costs with the houses.** Venus loses its power while the rail is rebuilt (standing with Venus falls). Earth
hires Mercury's helots as its contract hands, and they go home (standing with Earth falls). Every house that keeps a
caste reads one more broken world (ENDGAME.md, section 3, the broken-count table).

**If you play Mercury and break a chain anywhere** *(default, open)*: Mercury runs on helots more than any house but
Venus and Jupiter, so the price is high. Home income drops for three terms and the ephors put your King of the Field on
trial (a court-revolt event). Breaking Lakedaimon itself costs the same and fires MER-C2 at home at once.

**Losing Lakedaimon as Mercury** *(default, open)*: exile, not game over. A Mercury exile is a company without a city.
Your hired-out companies still pay you each term, which makes Mercury the easiest house to come back from exile with.

## E. The agoge and the Lochagos

Mercury has no blood enemy. A Mercury player's rival comes from whichever house first puts a contract out on them;
those lines live in that house's file. This section is Mercury's own school, and the rival Mercury sends to whoever is
the *target* of a Mercury contract.

**The school: the agoge.** It runs in the four rear cars of Lakedaimon, nearest the dawn, so every drill is done with
the heat at the trainees' backs. Children enter at seven and walk with the training cars, apart from their families.
Ship drill is flown from the rail itself: Aspis launch off a sled car and land back on a moving deck. Officers learn
contract law beside gunnery, because a captain who misreads a term by a minute has sold the company. The final test for
an officer is one term in command of a hired lochos against a lochos of their own classmates. It ends with the Dawn Run
like every child's.

**The rival: the Lochagos.** The top graduate of the agoge, a company captain, sent at the head of whichever Mercury
company is hired against you. It appears once any house hires Mercury against you (`flag(mercury-hired-against)`).
- Rolled names *(default, open)*: Brasidas, Kyniska, Gylippos, Agiatis. Lines use the title or they/them.
- Look: tall even for a Hemerodrome, a mirrored day mask pushed up on the forehead, a red-bronze cloak with one band at
  the hem in the current client's colour. The band changes with every contract.
- Flagship: the Hoplon *Anabasis*. Its domed shield is painted in the client's colour inside the gold rim.
- Wants: to fight one war that Mercury fights for itself.
- The lie: "The contract is clean. What it's for is the client's business."

**World 3, the raid** (hired by any house Hostile to you; escapes at term end)
- "One raid, paid in advance. Your newest world was on the order."
- "My client wanted it burning. It is burning."
- "Term's up. Bid next time, if you want me on your side."

**World 5, the Last Alliance** *(invented: commands the alliance's hired wing; the house rival leads the rest)*
- "Five houses pooled their money for me. It was a lot of money."
- "Outbid them and I turn now. You have until my Skytale lands."
- "No offer. Then we do this the expensive way."

**World 6, the last stand** (at Lakedaimon, fighting unpaid; or at the last capital if still under contract there)
- At Lakedaimon: "The city turned into the dawn for this. So did I."
- Elsewhere: "My client's walls. My contract. My last term."
- "Say less. Shoot."

**Outcomes**
- **Kill:** the shield comes home without its bearer. credits(+120), xp(+200), standing(mercury, -20), flag(lochagos-dead)
- **Capture** (drill boarder on the *Anabasis*): the ephors pay the ransom and never send the Lochagos out again.
  credits(+300), standing(mercury, +10), flag(lochagos-ransomed)
- **Turn** (Lakedaimon broken): the Lochagos asks no fee, the first contract of their life without one. pilots(+2),
  ships(capital, +1), flag(lochagos-turned)
- **Outbid** (not a turn; any time the Skytale tell shows): match the client's bid plus the margin and the Lochagos flies
  for you for one term, then goes back up for renewal. hire(lochos, 1), credits(-125)

## F. The Katabasis at Lakedaimon

1. **The fleet.** The King of the Field's Hoplon, one destroyer (LORE.md gives Mercury's destroyer no class name yet),
   two Embolon flown point-first, and four Aspis in a disc wall in front of the Hoplon's shield. Two Skytale runners
   leave at the first shot with the recall. Each Skytale that escapes brings one lochos home at stage 3. Companies
   under contract to anyone else, you included, keep their contracts and stay where they are paid to be.
2. **The armour.** The Kerykeion (GOD-ARMOUR.md): a straight band from pole to pole for the staff, two bands coiled round
   the planet across it for the snakes, crossing four times, a fan of feather plates over the north pole. All of it is
   built from overlapping round bronze shield plates with gold rims, and it covers about a third of the sphere. The
   armour is fixed to the planet; Lakedaimon walks under it. Break the guns on the stretch of band nearest the
   terminator and open a lane through the shield plates over the city's track. *(Gun placement is the stations
   thread's; this assumes guns on that stretch.)*
3. **The drop.** When the armour breaks, the ephors turn the city round. Lakedaimon runs east, into the dawn, at full
   rail speed. They are betting that citizens in mirrored masks can stand the edge of the day longer than your infantry
   can. Your carriers have to match a moving deck, land on the rear cars and fight forward while the dawn closes at
   twice walking pace. A heat clock runs from the moment the city turns. If the rail was cut in C, the clock starts at
   half.
4. **The palace.** The Two Thrones sit side by side in the centre car. Hold them through the counterattack. Holding
   Lakedaimon means holding its speed: the chains-or-broken prompt fires the moment the timer ends, because the rail
   gangs need an order before the next shift. Every Mercury company in the field ends its term and comes to you.

**The choice in the middle** (once the first carriers are down)
- Take the brake car: the city stops short of the dawn. The heat clock runs at half speed, and the brake car is lightly
  held. troops(-5)
- Take the Rail Car at the front and turn the city west again: the heat clock stops. The Rail Car is where the King of
  the Rail stands, and it costs the most to take. troops(-20)
- Leave both and push for the thrones: the full clock, and every trooper goes to the palace.

**The way to fail.** The dawn reaches the deck before the hold timer ends. Your infantry burn or fall back, the ephors
turn the city west, and Lakedaimon rebuilds its garrison. troops(-20), garrison(mercury-capital, +2)

## H. Endings

**The Sovereign.** Lakedaimon still runs west, and the helots still go out into the day to lay its rail. There is
nobody left to bid against you, so every company in the system flies on one contract, yours, and it never comes up
for renewal. The agoge still teaches contract law. Nobody can say what for.

**The Uneasy Crown.** The forward gang is paid and the day gang is not, and both know it. Kinadon's daughter is eight
and unedited, and the ephors have not decided whether she may ride the city. Companies still hire out to whoever
holds the free worlds. They watch the chained ones, waiting for a bid.

**The Breaker.** Lakedaimon still runs west; nobody is made to lay its rail. Some crews stay and are paid in power, and
some walk off into the dark to see what the night side holds. With no houses left, the companies have nobody to sell
to. Most of them come home and walk.

## New effects needed

- `hire(size, terms, sealed?)`: a Mercury company (`lochos` or `mora`) flies with the player for that many terms; with
  `sealed` it skips renewal and cannot be outbid.
- `hireAgainst(house, size)`: an AI house hires a Mercury company against the player; it fights in Mercury's colours
  under that house's standing, and sets `flag(mercury-hired-against)`.
- `turnCompany(house)`: a company the player hired ends its term and signs with `house`, turning where it stands.

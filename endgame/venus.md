# Venus: the Nephelai at the endgame

> DRAFT, 2026-10-20. One of eight files under `endgame/CONTRACT.md`. Built on LORE.md (Venus, "Who stands where"),
> FEUDS.md and ENDGAME.md; where this file and those disagree, they win. Game year is 6000, the Founding about 3000.
> numbers: FIRST PASS, for Palamedes review. All of them are collected in section I. Houses in effects are
> `earth moon mercury venus mars jupiter saturn`; worlds use the same ids.

## A. The house at the endgame

By the late game Venus has the smallest fleet that still calls itself a navy, and it knows it. One Palatine, the
Autokrator's own court ship, which rarely leaves the clouds. Two Dromons. Four Smaragdos, each painted by the family
that paid for it. LORE.md gives Venus no destroyer class, so two Siphon fire ships fill that slot. Whatever else it
fields is a Hemerodrome company hired that month, because Venus pays, marries or bribes before it fights.

Its fear is falling, and by now the fear is literal. Every city hangs from an envelope kept by people the court does
not count. Its want is to come out of the war as Earth's heir, with the Moon burned for the Nika night.

- **At the Reckoning (3 worlds).** Venus does not drift toward Hostile by shooting. It sends an envoy, a court title
  and a marriage offer, and pays a Mercury company to raid you under Mercury's flag.
- **In the Last Alliance (5).** Venus brings money, not ships. It pays the coalition's Mercury contracts and sends its
  Siphons to the boarding fights. The Palatine stays home. The Moon is in the same coalition and the blood feud still
  runs, so the Venus–Moon seam is where the alliance tears: hit one and the other will not cover it.
- **As your ally at the betrayal.** Venus is the ally most likely to sell you, and it sells for a title. The coalition
  offers it Pytho, and it opens your gates. If it stays loyal, its price is the Moon wiped out (its blood enemy), and
  it asks for a wedding on top.

## B. The caste: the Paroikoi

**Where the edit came from.** The acid in the clouds eats pumps, seals and seams faster than Earth could ship
replacements. In the first generation of the cloud cities, the families who held the lift contracts asked the first
Autokrator for people who could live inside the envelope and never leave it. The Autokrator signed the **Novel of the
Envelope** in purple ink: children born to envelope crews would be edited for the work and bound to the envelope of
the city they were born in. *Paroikoi* is the Byzantine word for tenants bound to a lord's land. Here the land is the
envelope, and the lord is whichever family holds that city's lift contract. When a family loses favour and loses the
contract (LORE.md), the Paroikoi go with the contract to the next family.

**What the edit does.** The envelope is full of breathable air, which floats in Venus's heavy carbon dioxide (outside
source). The crews live in its crown, where the air is kept thin and cold. *Chosen:* a deep chest, big lungs and thick
blood, so they can work a full shift in thin air, the way highland peoples on Earth can. Smaller and lighter even than
other Nephelai. Long fingers, gripping toes, and an inner ear edited to feel the city tilt before a gauge moves
(fiction). *Grown:* in the city's thick warm air they sleep badly and tire fast. Most of them have never stood inside
the palace they hold up.

**A day of work.** Wake in a hammock slung between two cell walls. Walk the seams with a lamp and a pot of sealant,
listening for the hiss of an acid pinhole. Patch it, mark it in chalk, move on. When a storm leans on the city, the
crown crews trim the cells by hand to keep the floor level, because below them the court is bowing in ranks and the
palace must not tilt. At the end of the shift, climb to the top of the envelope and eat in the open, above the clouds,
where nobody from the city ever goes.

**Their word for themselves.** The court and the census say *Paroikoi*, "the dependants". The crews call themselves
**Aerobatai**, "air-walkers", from the line Aristophanes gave Socrates in the *Clouds* (225), swinging in a basket
above the stage: "I walk on air." The court thinks the name is a joke about them. The crews know whose joke it is.

**Their saying.** "The palace hangs from us."

**What they want.** The lift contracts written in their own names, so they are paid for lift and own the cells they
keep. The right to come down into the city and stand in the Hippodrome. And for nobody to be sent down again.

**Two leaders**
- **Marina of the North Seam**, warden of the crown at Chrysopolis.
  - *Want:* the Chrysopolis lift contract written to the crews and not to a Blue family.
  - *Lie:* she says the failing seam at Chrysopolis is acid damage. It is her own crews' sealant, thinned for a year on
    her order, so the city would need its crews more than it needs Mercury's power.
- **Stephanos of the Ropes**, envelope-born, flies cargo tenders between the cities because the Magnaura will not take him.
  - *Want:* to fly a Smaragdos in no family's paint.
  - *Lie:* he says he has never been below the clouds. At twelve he struck a cadet, was sent down to Maxwell Anchor,
    and climbed the tether back up. Being found out means being sent down for life.

## C. The breaking point

LORE.md hook: "A Nephelan city is losing lift and asks Earth, not Mercury, for help." The city is **Chrysopolis**,
home of the Blue families. Asking Mercury means more debt to a house that despises Venus. Asking Earth means a foreign
fleet in Venus's sky and a family going over the Autokrator's head. **If you are Earth, the envoy comes to you.**

### VEN-BP1H: Chrysopolis Is Sinking
- Trigger: you hold Venus in chains, and either the Stirring unrest roll fires on Venus or `garrison(venus)` is below
  the garrison needed (section I).
- Read-aloud: Chrysopolis is lower than it was at dawn. You can see it from the palace windows, a gold dome sitting in
  the cloud tops. Its families have not written to you. They have sent an envoy to Earth.
- Choices:
  1. Buy pumping power from Mercury: credits(-200), standing(mercury, +5), card(VEN-BP2)
  2. Put troops in the envelope crown: troops(-2), garrison(venus, +2), unrest(venus, +10), card(VEN-BP2)
  3. Ignore it: flag(earth-in-chrysopolis), standing(earth, +5), income(venus, -15%), card(VEN-BP2)

### VEN-BP1A: A Letter Through Your Lines
- Trigger: you are at war with Venus, you do not hold it, worlds held ≥ 3.

  Chrysopolis families to Earth, begging for lift. A second letter is folded inside the first, in chalk, from the
  envelope crews.
- Choices:
  1. Let it pass to Earth: flag(chrysopolis-asked-earth), standing(earth, +5), card(VEN-BP2)

### VEN-BP2: The Crown Crews Stop
- Trigger: card VEN-BP1H or VEN-BP1A.

  1. Promise them the contract: flag(aerobatai-promised), unrest(venus, -20), standing(venus, -20), card(VEN-BP3)

- Trigger: card VEN-BP2 while you hold Venus; or `winWorld(venus)` while `aerobatai-promised` or `crown-sent-down` is

  3. Break them and let it sink: breakWorld(venus), loseOutpost(venus-outpost-1), pilots(+6), income(venus, -30%)

If Marina's lie comes out, `aerobatai-promised` still holds: the crews keep a promise made to them.

## D. Held and broken

**Held in chains.** The cities float and Venus pays its full income. It needs a garrison in two places, the envelope
crown and the Hippodrome, or unrest climbs each Stirring roll. Mercury's power bill comes due (VEN-04). Earth stays
Friendly toward a Venus held as it was. The Paroikoi give nothing beyond the lift.

**Broken.** One city sinks unless you pay to rebuild it (VEN-BP3). Venus's income drops while the contracts are
rewritten. The Aerobatai become the best pilots in the system and fly for you: a lump of pilots at once, more at each
Stirring, and a wing of Aerobates fighters.
- **Aerobates** (`venus-aerobates`): a Smaragdos with the gold claw stripped off, bare green glass, white seam-stitch
  marks down the fins. Stats are Palamedes's.
- Cost with the houses: Jupiter (a court that fears its own Laoi) and Earth (which courted the court) both cool.
- **If Venus is your home world**, breaking any world fires the court revolt (VEN-05) and cuts home income until you
  answer it. That is the price your house sets on breaking (default, open).

## E. The rival school and the rival

Venus's blood enemy is the Moon, so this rival hunts a **Selenite player**.

**The school: the Magnaura.** A domed hall under the Sacred Palace in Nephelokokkygia, named for the palace hall in
Constantinople that housed a school. Cadets are court children only; envelope-born are refused. They fly Smaragdos
round the outer course, between the tethers of the city's envelope, seven laps as in the old chariot races. A cadet
who touches a tether is sent home in disgrace. A cadet who touches the envelope is sent down. Graduates take a court
title and a family's paint.

**The rival, the Drungarios** (the Byzantine title for a fleet commander). Name rolled each game (default, open):
**Zoe Argyre**, **Theophano Skleraina**, **Ioannes Doukas**, **Basil Phokas**.
- *Look:* small even for a Nephelan, veiled in green silk, gold-leaf scale on the flight suit, rings on every finger.
  The rings hide rope calluses.
- *Flagship:* the Palatine **Chrysotriklinos**, named for the golden throne hall.
- *Want:* to stand in the imperial box at the Hippodrome and be acclaimed.
- *Lie they tell themselves:* "I owe the envelope nothing." The Drungarios was nursed in the Chrysopolis crown by a
  Paroikos nurse, as court children are, and learned to fly from envelope-born pilots in the tethers at night.

**Scene lines**
- *World 3, the raid* (`rivalScene(ven-w3)`)
  - "Your newest world. Venus keeps better books than the Moon does. I am here to balance them."
  - "You remember the Hippodrome. We remember it better."
  - "Count the fires before you count me."
- *World 5, leading the alliance* (`rivalScene(ven-w5)`)
  - "Every house you have not beaten is behind me. The Autokrator paid for the rest."
  - "The Mercury companies on your flank changed contracts this morning."
  - "Kneel at the Hippodrome and you keep your archive."
- *World 6, the last stand* (`rivalScene(ven-w6)`)
  - "If this world falls, it falls with me on it."
  - "We were raised to fear falling. I have practised."
  - "Come down, Selenite. The air is warm here."

**Endings**
- *Kill:* The Chrysotriklinos breaks up in the cloud deck and nothing of it comes back. credits(+120), xp(+200),
  standing(venus, -20), flag(rival-dead)
- *Capture:* Your boarders carry the Drungarios out of the throne hall, still veiled. The court pays to have them back.
  credits(+300), xp(+200), standing(venus, +10), flag(rival-captured)
- *Turn* (requires Venus broken): The Drungarios takes off the rings and shows you the calluses. They fly with you to
  the end. flag(rival-turned), ships(venus-palatine, +1), pilots(+2), standing(venus, -10)

## F. The Katabasis at Nephelokokkygia

1. **The fleet.** The Autokrator's Palatine, two Siphons in the destroyer slot, two Dromons and four Smaragdos, under
   the guns of Polykandelon. If Venus's standing with Mercury is Friendly or better, a hired Hemerodrome company
   flies with them: one Embolon and two Aspis. The Siphons hang back and wait for boarders.
2. **The armour: the Shell of Aphrodite** (GOD-ARMOUR.md). A scallop shell over the Earthward face, gold ribs fanning
   from a hinge, green glass between them, a ring of dove-wing plates round the hinge. The shield emitters sit on the
   dove-wing ring; break that ring and the glass between the ribs stops holding fire. The ribs carry the batteries.
   The Shell covers the Earthward face only, so the drop window opens when the city's drift carries it out from
   under the hinge (the upper winds go round Venus fast, outside source).
3. **The drop.** Infantry carriers go down into the cloud layer at about 50–55 km, through acid that eats hull for
   every second inside it. Below the cloud tops the city hangs under its envelope like a chandelier. There are two
   places to land: the Hippodrome, the only open floor in the city and next to the palace, or the envelope crown,
   where the Aerobatai live. The rival makes the last stand on the Hippodrome floor.
4. **The palace.** By Venus's own law, whoever holds the Sacred Palace and is acclaimed in the Hippodrome is crowned.
   So the infantry must hold both, the palace and the Hippodrome, through the counterattack. Holding them makes you
   Autokrator in Venus's own eyes, and the cities' families start bowing.

**The choice in the middle.** Land in the crown and you hold the knife: you can **cut the envelope**. The city falls
into the heat with the palace and the rival in it. You win the world at once, the rival is killed, and the
Aerobatai die with the envelope. The chains-or-broken prompt never comes, and Venus counts as chained. The capital
seat moves to Chrysopolis. If `aerobatai-promised` is set, the crown crews open the cell hatches and the crown landing
is unopposed; otherwise you fight them in the rigging.
- Cut effects: winWorld(venus), moveCapital(venus, venus-outpost-1), chainWorld(venus), income(venus, -60%),
  standing(earth, -15), standing(mercury, -15), standing(mars, -15), standing(jupiter, -15), standing(saturn, -15),
  standing(moon, +15), flag(nephelokokkygia-fallen), flag(rival-dead)

**The way to fail.** If the counterattack retakes the envelope crown while you hold the palace, the court vents the
lift cells rather than be taken. The city sinks below the cloud tops, every troop landed is lost, and the court refloats
it on reserve cells. You keep the space above it and start the drop again. If Venus is your home world and the
alliance holds it, this is where you are exiled from (default, open).

## H. Endings

**The Sovereign.** You sign in purple ink now. The cities float, the families keep their lift contracts, and the
contracts name you. The Aerobatai are still in the crown, patching seams for a palace they have never seen. Venus calls
you Autokrator and means it as long as you pay.

**The Uneasy Crown.** Chrysopolis floats lower than it used to and nobody will say why. Half the envelope crews have
contracts in their own names and half are waiting for theirs. The Hippodrome has three factions now, and the third
sits up in the cheap seats with chalk on its hands.

**The Breaker.** The Novel of the Envelope is burned in the Hippodrome. The sky over Venus is full of envelope-born
pilots flying in no family's paint, and the cities stay up because the people who keep them up own them. Nobody is
sent down.

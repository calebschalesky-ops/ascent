# Ascent Flight — a history of the solar system, 3000 to 6000

> **DRAFT, 2026-10-20.** Built on Caleb's ruling of 2026-10-20: **the game's present is the year 6000, and the
> Founding was around the year 3000.** That answers LORE.md's open question 2 ("How long ago was the Founding?") with
> three thousand years, not three hundred. Everything below is a first pass for Caleb to react to. Every name and date
> invented here can change; the ones taken from the sources are marked with a link to where they come from.
>
> History and myth only: no astrology, no planetary metals or days, no zodiac, no occult meaning. Themes are borrowed
> from Red Rising (castes, an orbital drop, betrayal, a rising from below); no name, term or line from those books is
> used. Every term is historical Greek, Roman or Byzantine, or LORE.md's own.

**Sources, in the order they win.** [LORE.md][L] (integration-3 worktree), [FEUDS.md][FEUDS], [ENDGAME.md][ENDGAME],
then the house files in [`endgame/`][EG]. Where this file had to invent, it invented inside them. Where it changes or
pins down something LORE.md says, the change is listed in the last section, "What this timeline changes in LORE.md".

**How dates are written.** Years are counted in the League calendar, which is Earth's ordinary calendar: the year the
Founding Charter was signed is 3000. Each house also keeps its own count, given where it first appears:

| House | Its own count | Year 6000 in it |
|---|---|---|
| Earth | The League calendar (this file's) | 6000 |
| Moon | Years of the Archive, from the first copy stored at Pytho in 3004; hours rung on Earth's 24-hour clock | 2996 of the Archive |
| Mercury | Circuits of the Rail, from the day the rail first closed round the planet in 3207; one circuit is one Mercury solar day, 4,222.6 hours (176 Earth days) | about circuit 5,796 |
| Venus | Years of the Throne, from the first Autokrator's acclamation in 3203 | 2797 of the Throne |
| Mars | Mars years (687 days) from the founding of Urbs Arsia in 3031, written *a.u.c.*, as Rome counted from its founding | about 1,578 a.u.c. |
| Jupiter | Callisto orbits (16.69 days) since the death of the founding governor in 3371, kept by the court | about 57,500 orbits |
| Saturn | Saturn years (29.46 Earth years), each opened by a Saturnalia; the Autochthones refuse to count from any date before the Silence (about 4100) | the 65th year of the Ekklesia's count |

**The rough shape.** Three thousand years is about 120 generations. That is still far too few for the bodies to have
changed by evolution, so every difference between the peoples is still *grown*, *chosen* or *custom*, as LORE.md
("On bodies") lays out. The edits were made by people, on dates, and this file gives the dates.

The four eras LORE.md names ([LORE, "A short history"][L-history]) are the Founding, the Spreading, the Editing and
the Sundering. They overlap: the first edit (3122) was made before Venus was settled, Titan's people were edited from
their first children, Saturn stopped answering (about 4100) before the last caste was edited (4371), and Jupiter's
crowns were founded in the Spreading. The dates above mark when each process was the main thing happening. "Before the
Founding", "the Age of the Seven" and "the last fifty years" are this file's additions, needed once three thousand
years have to be filled.

## 2. Before the Founding (2961–2999)

### 2961 — The Quito tether is begun
- **What happened.** A consortium of Earth's equatorial nations begins the space elevator on the equator in the Andes,
  2,850 m up, at the place that becomes Quito Skyport. The equator gives the tether a straight climb; the altitude puts
  a third of the air below the base.
- **Who.** Eleven nations, later the core of the League.
- **Consequences.** The cost of lifting a tonne to orbit falls far enough for colonies to be paid for. Every colony
  ship of the next three thousand years leaves from the top of this tether.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Bases][L-earth] (Quito Skyport, Pharos Station).

### 2987 — The Metropolitan League is formed
- **What happened.** Earth's nations, "working together for once", form a league on the Achaean model: each keeps its
  flag and its vote in a council, the **Synedrion**, and they pool one navy under one **Strategos**, elected for a
  year. The League's stated purpose is to found and supply colonies.
- **Who.** Forty-one founding member nations. The first Synedrion sits at the foot of the tether.
- **Consequences.** The League "was formed to run the Founding and outlived it". The rule that a Strategos may not serve
  two years running dates from this charter.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Society; Government][L-earth].

### 2989 — Pharos Station opens
- **What happened.** The tether reaches orbit. The ring-and-spoke wheel at its top, brushed steel with blue window
  bands, is named Pharos after the lighthouse of Alexandria.
- **Consequences.** It is "the lighthouse every colony ship sailed from". Its lamp is lit on every Founding Day.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Bases][L-earth].

### 2994 — The first Ark is laid down at Lagrange Yard
- **What happened.** Lagrange Yard, the League's shipyard ring, lays down the first Ark: a fat brass hull round a
  turning habitat ring, unarmed. The ring spins to give a full gravity on the way out, so that colonists arrive able to
  walk.
- **Consequences.** The Ark design is still built in 6000, still at Lagrange Yard. The spinning ring is the first form
  of the Return Clause's nursery rings.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Ship classes (Ark)][L-earth].

## 3. The Founding (3000–3164)

The League founds two colonies, the Moon and Mars, and learns what low gravity does to children.

### 3000 — The Charter of the Apoikiai
- **What happened.** The League signs one charter for all colonies. Each settlement is an *apoikia*, "a home away", and
  Earth is the *metropolis*, the mother city. Three clauses matter later:
  1. **The Honour Clause.** "A colony owes the mother city honour." It does not say obedience. The founders took the
     wording from the Greek colonies without thinking hard about what it would mean in three thousand years.
  2. **The Return Clause.** Every child born in an apoikia is to be raised able to stand and walk on Earth, so that any
     colonist can come home.
  3. **The Lunar Schedule.** The Moon is listed as League territory "held in common", unlike the other colonies, which
     are listed as apoikiai. This is the clause a Selenite archivist finds again in the game (Moon hook 3).
- **Who.** The Synedrion, unanimously. The Charter is read aloud at Quito on the first Founding Day.
- **Consequences.** Founding Day begins: every member nation lights a lamp for the colonies it sent out. The Honour
  Clause is the root of every Sundering. The Return Clause holds for 122 years and then fails.
- **Supports.** [LORE, "The Founding"][L-history]; [LORE, Earth: Culture (Founding Day)][L-earth]; Moon hook 3.

### 3003 — The first permanent settlement on the Moon
- **What happened.** Two hundred settlers land at Copernicus crater on the near side. They are there to build an
  observatory and to keep the **Deep Copy**, a second copy of Earth's records, held off-world in case Earth's are lost.
- **Consequences.** The Moon is the archive from its first year. "The biggest archive off Earth" starts here.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Society][L-moon].

### 3004 — Pytho is founded; the Archive count begins
- **What happened.** The first copy of the Deep Copy is stored under the crater wall. The settlement takes the name
  **Pytho**, the old name of Delphi. The settlers note that the Earth never moves in the near-side sky, and call the
  spot under it the *omphalos*, the navel of the world.
- **Consequences.** The Selenitai count years of the Archive from here. The Moon keeps Earth's 24-hour clock from the
  first night, and ringing the hours becomes a duty before it becomes a religion.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Society; Bases][L-moon].

### 3011 — The Clarke Belt Relays are finished
- **What happened.** A ring of relay stations in geostationary orbit begins carrying every message between Earth and
  its colonies.
- **Consequences.** Whoever holds the relays hears the system. In the Sundering, Saturn's Silence is first noticed here.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Bases][L-earth].

### 3019 — The Peaks of Light
- **What happened.** Solar towers are raised on the rim of Shackleton crater at the lunar south pole, on peaks that are
  almost always lit, over craters that are always dark and hold ice.
- **Consequences.** The Moon has its own power and water. Ice mining in the cold traps begins.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Bases][L-moon]; [CONQUEST.md, Moon][CONQ].

### 3026 — The first landing on Mars
- **What happened.** The first Mars expedition lands on the flank of Arsia Mons and goes underground into the lava
  tubes, because Mars has no magnetic field to turn the radiation.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Bodies; Bases][L-mars].

### 3031 — Urbs Arsia is founded
- **What happened.** The settlement in the Arsia tubes is chartered as an apoikia. Its people light the tubes through
  the skylights where the roofs fell in. They name it *Urbs*, "the City", and count years from this one.
- **Who.** The first colonial governor, appointed by the League; a colonial council of heads of household advises.
- **Consequences.** The Mars year (687 days) count *a.u.c.* begins. Mars is the only colony with a day close to
  Earth's (24.66 hours) and keeps living by the Sun.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Society; Bases][L-mars].

### 3040 — The first Pathfinder
- **What happened.** Lagrange Yard launches the Pathfinder, a brass courier: a lathed cigar with swept wings. It carries
  mail, medicine and passengers between Earth, the Moon and Mars.
- **Consequences.** Every later colony ship, and in the end every house's cruiser line, descends from its hull. In
  6000 it is still the ship an Earth player flies.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Ship classes (Pathfinder)][L-earth].

### 3058 — The Long Children
- **What happened.** The first generation born on the Moon reaches school age. At 0.165 g they have grown long, with
  thin bones and small hearts. Brought to Earth for the Return test, most cannot stand.
- **Who.** Sixty-four Moon-born children, flown to Quito; the photographs are still in Earth's museums.
- **Consequences.** The League enforces the Return Clause with **nursery rings**: spinning habitats in which colonial
  children spend most of their first fifteen years at a full gravity. They are expensive and children hate them. The
  colonies call it "the Spin". It lasts sixty-four years on the Moon (section 5, 3122).
- **Supports.** [LORE, "The Editing"][L-history] ("children born on the Moon could not stand on Earth").

### 3071 — The Spin comes to Mars
- **What happened.** Mars's first nursery ring is built in orbit near Phobos.
- **Consequences.** Martian children grow up between a ring at 1 g and a home at 0.38 g. The ring's site becomes Castra
  Phobos in the Secession.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Bases (Castra Phobos)][L-mars].

### 3094 — The College of the Pythia
- **What happened.** The League cuts the Deep Copy's budget. The archivists at Pytho begin to answer questions from any
  nation for a fee: what a record says, what a treaty meant, how a dispute was settled before. They organise as a
  college of about three hundred, chosen by examination, and elect a head for life to speak for them. They call her the
  **Pythia**.
- **Consequences.** The Moon starts earning its own money. The Pythia's answers are scholarship, not prophecy (LORE.md
  open question 14), and their worth rests on never lying in an answer. The rule that an archivist caught forging a
  record is exiled to Earth dates from the first such case, in 3121.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Society; Government; Culture][L-moon].

### 3110 — The Hall of Answers
- **What happened.** The College cuts the Hall of Answers under the near side, its roof open to the Earth overhead.
  "Know yourself" and "Nothing in excess" are carved over the entrance.
- **Consequences.** It is the seat of the Selenite state until 6000.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Look; Culture][L-moon]; [CONQUEST.md, Moon (the Omphalos)][CONQ].

### 3120 — Aqua Borealis is begun
- **What happened.** Mars starts an aqueduct to carry melted ice from the north polar cap south to the cities.
- **Consequences.** It takes ninety years. It is the first work of what becomes the Greening, and the first place
  Martian children are taken on the long walk to see the sky.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Bases][L-mars].

### 3136 — The Comitia of Mars
- **What happened.** Mars's colonial council becomes an assembly of every adult, meeting by work-gang. The governor
  still rules; the assembly advises and votes the colony's own taxes.
- **Consequences.** The habit of citizens meeting in their units becomes the assembly "in their legions" of the
  Republic.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Government][L-mars].

### 3164 — The Founding Board is wound up
- **What happened.** The League's Founding Board, which ran the Moon and Mars colonies, is closed. Colonial affairs pass
  to the Synedrion as a standing committee.
- **Consequences.** The League has done what it was formed to do, and stays. From here it is Earth's government.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Society][L-earth].

## 4. The Spreading (3165–3599)

"Venus's cloud cities came next, then the walking city on Mercury, then the stations on Jupiter's outer moons, and last
Titan" ([LORE, "The Spreading"][L-history]).

### 3168 — The first aerostat over Venus
- **What happened.** A League survey hangs a habitat in the cloud layer at about 52 km, where (outside source) the
  pressure and temperature are near Earth's surface.
- **Consequences.** Venus is the colony that is easiest on bodies: 0.91 g. Its children could have passed the Return
  test without the Spin, but by 3168 the Moon has already dropped the Return Clause, and Venus edits from its second
  generation for size and acid instead (section 5, 3205).
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Bodies][L-venus].

### 3176 — The Mercury survey
- **What happened.** A League survey finds that Mercury's terminator moves at about 3.6 km/h at the equator, walking
  pace, and proposes a city that rides rails in the twilight and never stops.
- **Consequences.** The Cold Wells are opened in the north polar craters for water.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: The walking city; Bases][L-mercury].

### 3184 — Nephelokokkygia is founded
- **What happened.** The first full cloud city is lifted. Its builders name it *Nephelokokkygia*, Cloud-cuckoo-land,
  after the city in the sky in Aristophanes' *Birds*, as a joke at their own expense. The League's clerks enter it in the
  register under that name, and it sticks.
- **Who.** The first Exarch of Venus, the League's governor, and the lift engineers.
- **Consequences.** The city hangs under its lifting envelope like a chandelier. "Nothing is ever thrown away" begins
  as a rule about mass: every kilo has to be lifted.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Name; Bases][L-venus].

### 3192 — Lakedaimon begins to walk; the Great Rhetra
- **What happened.** The first section of Lakedaimon is set on rails in the twilight band. The rail does not yet go
  round the planet; crews lay it ahead of the city and lift it from behind. The settlers write their founding law, the
  **Great Rhetra**: two kings (one for the rail, one for the field), each office hereditary in one family, and five
  ephors elected each Mercury year to watch them.
- **Who.** The first King of the Rail, the rail-master who designed the city; the first King of the Field, commander
  of its guard; the first five ephors.
- **Consequences.** In the same founding year the first King of the Rail asks the first ephors whether to send
  citizens into the day and lose them, or make people who could stand it. The ephors vote for the second: the **helot
  edit** (section 5, 3192). Laying rail ahead of the city becomes the work the whole planet depends on.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: The walking city; Government][L-mercury]; [ENDGAME §2][E-castes];
  [endgame/mercury.md §B][X-mercury].

### 3196 — The Hippodrome of Nephelokokkygia
- **What happened.** The Exarch builds a racing track in a ring round the city's lower dome, for crews off shift.
  Racing clubs form; by 3220 the two biggest are the **Greens** and the **Blues**.
- **Consequences.** Public life on Venus runs through the clubs. They are parties within a century.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Society][L-venus].

### 3203 — The first Autokrator
- **What happened.** The Exarch of Venus, **Anna Kallergis**, is acclaimed *Autokrator* in the Hippodrome by both
  factions after she keeps the city aloft through an acid storm that tore three lift cells. The League, months away,
  accepts the title as a local style for its governor, and keeps confirming each new Autokrator for nearly thirteen centuries.
- **Consequences.** The **Sacred Palace** and its court of titles and purple ink begin here. Venus counts Years of the
  Throne from 3203. The rule that whoever holds the palace and is acclaimed in the Hippodrome is crowned dates from
  Kallergis's own acclamation.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Society; Government][L-venus]; [endgame/venus.md §B][X-venus] ("the first Autokrator").

### 3207 — The Closing of the Rail
- **What happened.** The rail closes round the planet, about 15,330 km. Lakedaimon can now walk west forever on track
  that only needs repair, and helots to repair it.
- **Consequences.** Mercury counts circuits from here. Most of the rail lies in the full day at 590–725 K at any hour,
  where it warps; the city meets it again when the night brings it round. The helots' work never ends.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: The walking city][L-mercury]; [endgame/mercury.md §B][X-mercury].

### 3213 — The first Dawn Run
- **What happened.** A rail crew's children, trained to cross the terminator on foot ahead of the sunrise as a safety
  drill, make the run as a race. It becomes the end of every child's training.
- **Consequences.** The *agoge*, the training in endurance and discipline, is formalised by 3240. It runs in the four
  rear cars of the city, nearest the dawn, so every drill is done with the heat at the trainees' backs. Children enter at
  seven. Its fort-school, **the Agoge**, rides with the city.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: Culture (the Dawn Run)][L-mercury]; [endgame/mercury.md §E][X-mercury];
  [CONQUEST.md, Mercury][CONQ].

### 3215 — The Beam Compact
- **What happened.** Mercury's day-side Beam Towers begin selling power to Venus by beam. Venus's cloud cities need
  power to stay up; Mercury, with sunlight 6.674 times Earth's, has more than it can use.
- **Consequences.** Venus has bought most of Mercury's power ever since. Mercury's contempt for Venus, for needing it, is
  as old as the contract. Every beam sold crosses rail the helots laid, which is the root of what the helots want in
  6000: a share of the power.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: Earth and the others][L-mercury]; [LORE, Venus][L-venus];
  [endgame/mercury.md §B][X-mercury].

### 3302 — Chrysopolis is lifted
- **What happened.** The second cloud city is lifted, trailing the capital.
- **Consequences.** Chrysopolis becomes the city of the great families who back the Blues. Nephelokokkygia backs the
  Greens.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Bases][L-venus].

### 3330 — The Jovian Expedition
- **What happened.** The League sends a military governor with four fleet generals to found stations on Jupiter's outer
  moons, where (outside source) the radiation is milder. The governor is **Arden Hale**.
- **Who.** Governor Hale; generals **Danel Arkas** (later called *Soter*), **Mira Castell** (*Nikator*), **Ivo Brandt**
  (*Keraunos*) and **Selin Varga** (*Poliorketes*). These are the four founders whose statues stand on every Jovian
  skyline.
- **Consequences.** The Jovian colonies are run as a command, not a civilian charter. A governor with generals is the
  seed of the crowns.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Society][L-jupiter].

### 3339 — Kallistopolis is founded
- **What happened.** Hale's capital is cut into the Valhalla basin on Callisto, outside the worst of Jupiter's radiation
  (outside source), under ice domes.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Where they live; Bases][L-jupiter].

### 3352 — Ganymedeia
- **What happened.** Mira Castell founds the second seat on Ganymede, the largest moon in the system.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Bases][L-jupiter].

### 3361 — The Europa quarries and the Io pits
- **What happened.** Ivo Brandt opens ice quarries on Europa; Selin Varga opens sulphur pits on Io, in the worst of the
  radiation. Free workers will not stay, so Hale sends convicts from all four moons, and Varga builds the **Io
  Prison-fort** over the lift head.
- **Consequences.** Io is the most dangerous place anyone works in the system. Convicts sent there die inside two
  years. Forty years later their children are the first in the Jovian system to be edited for the work (3401).
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Where they live][L-jupiter]; [CONQUEST.md, Jupiter][CONQ].

### 3371 — The death of Arden Hale
- **What happened.** Governor Hale dies at Kallistopolis without naming an heir. Each general holds one moon. A story
  goes round the Io pits within the year that Hale had a son by a convict woman and hid him below. Nobody can prove it,
  and the crowns have hanged people for repeating it.
- **Consequences.** The **Diadoch Wars** begin. The court's count of Callisto orbits starts from the day of the death.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Society][L-jupiter] ("The founding governor ... died without naming an heir").

### 3371–3398 — The Diadoch Wars
- **What happened.** The four generals fight for Hale's command. Each crowns themself *Basileus* of their moon in turn,
  Danel Arkas first, in 3374. The League, an hour away by light and months away by ship, recognises all four as
  "governors-royal" rather than fight them.
- **Who.** The four founders and their Companions, the noble pilots who fly with them.
- **Consequences.** Four crowns, none strong enough to take the others. Hetairos fighters, flown by nobles, are first
  built in this war. Selin Varga earns the epithet *Poliorketes*, "the besieger", breaking Ganymede's ice works with a
  siege tower of a ship, the first Helepolis.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Society; Ship classes (Hetairos, Helepolis)][L-jupiter].

### 3398 — The Peace of the Four Moons
- **What happened.** The four crowns agree borders: one moon each. Every crown keeps the right to send its convicts to
  Io. The moon is called **Tartaros** from here.
- **Consequences.** Four kingdoms, each still calling itself a governorate-royal of the League. The rumour of Hale's
  hidden son outlives every one of the founders. (Jupiter hook 2 is built on it.)
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Bases (Tartaros)][L-jupiter]; Jupiter hook 2.

### 3438 — The first Tessarakonteres
- **What happened.** Callisto launches a flagship larger than anything Earth has built, named for the giant galley of the
  successor kings.
- **Consequences.** Every Jovian flagship since has been built to outdo the last. Ganymede answers in 3449.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Culture (Arts); Ship classes][L-jupiter].

### 3440 — The Royal Pages
- **What happened.** Danel Arkas's grandson founds a school under the oldest ice dome of the Basileia of Callisto. The
  children of Companions are taken at fourteen. They serve the king at table, guard the king's sleep in turns and fly
  as the king's wing in battle. The final test, the **Night Watch**, is one full orbit of Callisto alone at the king's
  door with a live sword.
- **Consequences.** The Royal Pages is Jupiter's officer school and, in the endgame, the rival's school when Jupiter is
  the player's blood enemy. Every page is raised by a Laos nurse taken from the Io hostages (section 5, 3401).
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §4, the rival][E-rival]; [endgame/jupiter.md §E][X-jupiter].

### 3451 — First Crown and Second Crown stations
- **What happened.** Callisto builds a crown-shaped station off its moon: a dark core ring with four upswept prongs, one
  for each founder. Ganymede builds its own in 3453, a little taller, on purpose.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Bases][L-jupiter].

### 3478 — The Titan expedition is staged from Callisto
- **What happened.** The League charters a colony on Titan. Because Callisto is the last port before Saturn, the
  expedition is fitted out there. A third of its crews are Jovian-born, hired from the House of Callisto, and Callisto
  supplies the fuel.
- **Consequences.** This is the factual root of the **cousins** claim. The Diadochoi can show that their people helped
  found Titan; the Autochthones say they came from nowhere.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Earth and the others]; [LORE, Jupiter][L-jupiter]; [FEUDS §3, claim feuds][F-feuds].

### 3512 — Landfall on Titan
- **What happened.** The colonists land on the shore of Kraken Mare. Air at about 1.6 times Earth's pressure means no
  pressure suits; at −179 °C they need warmth and oxygen. A message to Earth takes more than an hour.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Where they live][L-saturn].

### 3520 — Astu is founded
- **What happened.** The settlement on Kraken Mare's shore is chartered. The colonists hold their first meeting on a
  hill above the sea and call the hill the **Pnyx** after the one in Athens.
- **Consequences.** Titan's settlers meet as a whole from the first year, because there are only four hundred of them.
  Their children are edited from the first birth (section 5, 3514): by 3512 the Return Clause is dead everywhere.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bases][L-saturn]; [CONQUEST.md, Saturn (the Pnyx)][CONQ].

### 3528 — The first flight on Titan
- **What happened.** A settler straps on wings and flies, using the thick air and low gravity together.
- **Consequences.** Flying to the assembly becomes custom within a generation. Tall towers to jump from go up in every
  settlement.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bodies; Look][L-saturn].

### 3533 — The Ligeia Rigs
- **What happened.** Floating rigs on Ligeia Mare begin pumping hydrocarbons.
- **Consequences.** Titan has the system's fuel. The first **Holkas** tankers carry it inward in 3561.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bases; Ship classes (Holkas)][L-saturn].

### 3558 — The Prytaneion at Enceladus
- **What happened.** A station is built off Enceladus, by the geysers, to take water for Titan.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bases][L-saturn].

### 3574 — Stoa Station
- **What happened.** A colonnade of docks at the rings, where skimmers unload ring ice.
- **Consequences.** The ring ice everyone later wants is first sold from here.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bases][L-saturn].

### 3590 — The last charter
- **What happened.** The League's colonial committee closes its last open charter. No new colony is chartered for 1,812
  years, until Oberon (5402).
- **Supports.** Earth hook 3 (the background in section 11).

## 5. The Editing (3122–4371)

"Bodies built for Earth broke down in low gravity ... The colonies chose, one by one, to edit their children for the
worlds they lived on. Earth did not" ([LORE, "The Editing"][L-history]).

The Editing came in two waves. The **first editing** fitted each people to its world: bones, eyes, skin, lungs. It ran
from 3122 to 3514. The **second editing** made the castes: "Each colony edited its ruling families for command and its
workers for the work" ([ENDGAME §2][E-castes]). It ran from 3192, when Mercury edited its rail crews, to 4371, when
Titan voted the Metic edit. Mars did both at once, late, while it armed for war.

### 3122 — The Vote of the Long Bones
- **What happened.** After sixty-four years of the Spin, the Moon's settlers vote to stop sending their children to the
  nursery rings and to edit them instead. The edit stops their bones thinning past a set floor. It is kept short of
  what Earth's gravity would need, on purpose: the settlers say a child edited to stand on Earth will be sent there.
- **Who.** The Moon's settler assembly, on a motion from the College of the Pythia.
- **Consequences.** The Return Clause is broken for the first time, and the League does nothing to enforce it. Every
  colony follows within four centuries, except Mars, which keeps the Spin longest (4296). The Selenitai still cannot
  walk on Earth without a frame.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Bodies][L-moon] ("they never went that far, on purpose").

### 3131 — The Quito Declaration of the Unedited Body
- **What happened.** The Synedrion votes that no child of a member nation will ever be edited. It does not forbid the
  colonies to edit theirs; it says Earth will not.
- **Who.** The Synedrion, thirty-nine nations to two.
- **Consequences.** "That is where 'human' and 'the others' began." The unedited body becomes Earth's shared belief
  across all its faiths, and its pride. It also leaves Earth unable to work its own orbital yards for long, which leads
  straight to the Contract of Pharos (3200) and, 2,800 years later, to the Ark (5940).
- **Supports.** [LORE, "The Editing"][L-history]; [LORE, Earth: Bodies; Culture][L-earth];
  [endgame/earth.md §B][X-earth].

### 3192 — The helot edit and the glare edit
- **What happened.** In Lakedaimon's founding year the ephors vote two edits. Every child gets the **glare edit**:
  the darkest skin of any people and a second clear eyelid against the light. The children of the rail crews, and only
  they, also get the **heat edit**: bodies that hold a core temperature that would kill a citizen, cells that repair
  radiation damage in sleep, a thicker second eyelid, a short broad build for lifting rail. The heat edit is written
  into the city's law as property of the Rail.
- **Who.** The first King of the Rail put the question; the first five ephors voted.
- **Consequences.** The **helots** are made. They call themselves *Hemerobatai*, the day-walkers. "Helots" is Mercury's
  word, and the mercenary companies carry it through the system as slang after 4411.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Mercury][E-castes]; [endgame/mercury.md §B][X-mercury]; [LORE, Mercury: Bodies][L-mercury].

### 3200 — The Contract of Pharos
- **What happened.** Earth's yards, Pharos Station and the Clarke Belt Relays run in free fall or spin, where unedited
  bones thin within a year. The Synedrion votes its first labour treaty: Earth will hire the bodies it refuses to make.
  Moon settlers bound by debt for their passage take the long free-fall shifts. Mercury's helots take the hot work on
  the sunward frames.
- **Who.** The Synedrion. Every Strategos since has signed the Contract in the first week of office. Nobody reads it.
- **Consequences.** The **contract hands** begin. They live their whole contracts in orbit and never go down the
  tether. They call themselves **the Ink**, because the only part of them that reaches Quito is a signature. Earth's
  claim to be the only free people is half a lie from this year on ([ENDGAME §2][E-castes]).
- **Supports.** [endgame/earth.md §B][X-earth] (`contractOfPharosYear: 3200`).

### 3205 — The Nephelan edit
- **What happened.** Venus's second generation is edited small and light, because every kilo has to be lifted, with
  lungs and skin that stand the acid in the air (outside source: the clouds are sulphuric acid).
- **Consequences.** The Nephelai keep the most Earth-like bodies of any edited people. Only they can walk on Earth
  unaided, and they make a point of it. It is the root of their claim to be Earth's true heirs.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Bodies; Earth and the others][L-venus].

### 3211 — The Novel of the Envelope
- **What happened.** The acid eats pumps, seals and seams faster than Earth can ship replacements. The families who
  hold the lift contracts ask Autokrator Anna Kallergis for people who can live inside the lifting envelope and never
  leave it. She signs the **Novel of the Envelope** in purple ink: children of envelope crews are to be edited for the
  work (deep chest, big lungs, thick blood for thin cold air; smaller and lighter even than other Nephelai; long
  fingers, gripping toes, an inner ear that feels the city tilt) and bound to the envelope of the city they were born in.
- **Consequences.** The **Paroikoi**, "the dependants", are made. They call themselves *Aerobatai*, "air-walkers",
  after Aristophanes' Socrates in his basket. When a family loses favour and its lift contract, its Paroikoi pass with
  the contract to the next family.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Venus][E-castes]; [endgame/venus.md §B][X-venus].

### 3345 — The Jovian edit
- **What happened.** Governor Hale orders the first children born on Callisto edited for the dim light (0.037 of
  Earth's): very large eyes, the most sensitive of any people, pale skin, and radiation-repair genes (fiction).
- **Consequences.** The Diadochoi cannot stand full sunlight. Grown in 0.13 g, they are the tallest people in the
  system.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Bodies][L-jupiter].

### 3393–3398 — The Quito Crisis
- **What happened.** Two blocs of League nations quarrel over the tolls on the Quito tether and come close to war. Both
  move their gold reserves and their national records off Earth, out of each other's reach, to the one place every
  nation trusts: the Moon's archive. The other nations follow.
- **Consequences.** The system's money starts to sleep under the Moon. The quarrel is settled in 3398 by the rule that
  the tether is League property, not any nation's.
- **Supports.** [endgame/moon.md §B][X-moon] ("when every nation was moving its gold and its records off Earth").

### 3399 — The Treasuries and the Amphictyony
- **What happened.** Vaults are dug under the crust beside Pytho, one for each nation that asks, each built in its
  owner's style. The vault keeper of the Venus treasury works under gold mosaic; the keeper of the Mars vault, when there
  is one, under bare concrete arches. The vaults are sealed in thin, cold, dry air so paper and film keep and nothing can
  burn. To guard them, the depositors form the **Amphictyony**, a council in which every depositing nation has a seat.
- **Consequences.** The Moon becomes the system's bank as well as its archive. The Amphictyony at this date is a
  council of Earth's member nations and the colonies; it becomes a council of the seven houses at the Compact of the
  Sanctuary (4612).
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Society; Bases (the Treasuries)][L-moon]; [endgame/moon.md §B][X-moon].

### 3401 — The Laoi edit at the Io Prison-fort
- **What happened.** The convicts sent to Io's sulphur pits die inside two years. The four crowns meet at the Io
  Prison-fort and agree one thing together: the pits will never stop. They order the convicts' children edited to live
  in the radiation: grey, thick skin that scars instead of burning; short and dense for tunnels; small dark eyes for
  lamp-light; far more radiation repair than any Diadoch, which holds for a working life and then fails all at once.
- **Who.** All four founders' heirs. It is the only law all four crowns ever signed.
- **Consequences.** The **Laoi**, "the folk", are made. Every gang gives one child to the ice palaces as a servant,
  nurse or cup-bearer, as a guarantee that the gang keeps digging. The gangs call themselves *Homeroi*, the hostages.
  Every Royal Page from now on is raised by a Laos nurse.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Jupiter][E-castes]; [endgame/jupiter.md §B][X-jupiter].

### 3404 — The Hierodoulos edit
- **What happened.** Ordinary Selenites faint in the vault air within the hour. The College proposes an edit for the
  children who will keep the vaults: longer bones than any other Selenite, to reach the top of racks cut for columns
  that could never stand on Earth, and a deep chest and slow breath for air as thin as a mountain top. The Amphictyony
  votes for it and pays for it, every seat. The first edited children are born to families who still owe for their
  passage up from Earth; the Amphictyony buys the debt, and the children inherit it with their lungs.
- **Consequences.** The **Hierodouloi**, the temple's servants, are made. A Hierodoulos cannot leave the Moon, so the
  law that exiles a forger to Earth is a death sentence for them. A sixth of each keeper's wage goes on the debt, and
  among themselves they are the *Hektemoroi*, the sixth-parters. From 3404 the "Moon hands" under the Contract of Pharos
  are mostly debtors sold on by the Treasuries.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Moon][E-castes]; [endgame/moon.md §B][X-moon] (`casteEditedYear: 3400`).

### 3460–3720 — The command edits
- **What happened.** The ruling families of every colony except Saturn edit their own children for command. The
  Companions of the Jovian crowns take reflex and spatial edits for flight; the great families of Venus take an edit
  for the court's endless ceremony (stamina in standing, a steadier voice); the two royal lines of Mercury take an
  edit for heat tolerance short of the helots'. Each edit is kept inside the family by marriage law.
- **Consequences.** By 3720 rank in every colony but Titan is written in the body as well as in law. Saturn's
  assembly votes three times against any edit of rank, which is the root of its pride in equality.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2][E-castes] ("edited its ruling families for command").

### 3488 — The Stopped Shift (Mercury)
- **What happened.** The forward helot gang, short of water after a Cold Wells failure, refuses to go out ahead of the
  city. Lakedaimon comes within forty hours of the dawn. The King of the Field takes citizens into the dark to lay
  rail; eleven die of the cold. The gang's foremen are executed.
- **Consequences.** The ephors set up the **krypteia**, a secret watch on the helots drawn from the agoge's best,
  which still exists in 6000. Every later helot rising starts by killing its krypteia.
- **Supports.** The background of Mercury hook 1 ([LORE, Mercury: Plot hooks][L-mercury]); [ENDGAME §2][E-castes].

### 3514 — The Autochthon edit
- **What happened.** Titan's first children are edited for the cold and the dark: very large dark eyes, heat-holding
  bodies with thick fat and dense fur-like body hair (fiction), and light enough, with the thick air and low gravity, to
  fly on strapped-on wings.
- **Consequences.** Flight becomes the mark of a citizen. Four centuries later the Metic edit is made too heavy to fly
  (4371), and the difference becomes the sign of who votes.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Bodies][L-saturn]; [endgame/saturn.md §B][X-saturn].

### 3612 — The Envelope Rising (Venus)
- **What happened.** The Paroikoi of the cloud city **Aerope**, bound to a family that has cut their rations, close the
  crown valves and hold the city level only on their own terms. The Autokrator sends Hemerodrome-trained guards up the
  tethers. The leaders are taken alive and **sent down**: lowered to the surface works over Maxwell Montes, the highest
  and coolest ground on Venus.
- **Consequences.** Being sent down becomes the worst punishment on Venus. The surface works become **Maxwell Anchor**,
  where the sent-down work. Aerope survives this rising and is lost in the Nika Burning (4791).
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Culture; Bases (Maxwell Anchor)][L-venus]; [CONQUEST.md, Venus (the Descent)][CONQ].

### 3617 — The Fall of the House of Io
- **What happened.** The Laoi rise in the pits. They hold the lift for nineteen days and kill the last king of Selin
  Varga's line when he comes down to treat with them. The Houses of Callisto and Ganymede put the rising down together
  and take Io jointly.
- **Consequences.** Three crowns remain. Io is ruled from the Prison-fort by a warden appointed by both houses in turn.
  The Laoi learn that the crowns will stop fighting each other to keep the pits working. The story of Hale's hidden son
  in the pits gains a second form: that Varga's line hid it.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Society ("Two kingdoms remain strong")][L-jupiter]; Jupiter hook 2.

### 4296 — Mars gives up the Spin
- **What happened.** Mars is the last colony to keep its children in nursery rings, because it wants citizens who
  could fight on Earth if they had to. By 4296 it can no longer afford the rings and arm at the same time. The colonial
  assembly votes an edit that keeps bones and heart close to human and edits the eyes for low light.
- **Who.** The Comitia, under the League's governor, who signs it.
- **Consequences.** Quirites grow tall and long-limbed but keep near-human bones and hearts. They are the only edited
  people built with Earth in mind as a battlefield.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Bodies][L-mars] ("because Mars meant to fight Earth if it had to").

### 4331 — The Censors' Edict
- **What happened.** Arming for a war with Earth, Mars needs ice faster than its citizens will dig it. The first
  censors, a new colonial office for the census, write a second, harsher edit for the families who work the buried
  glacier under Arsia Mons and own nothing else: eyes past the citizen edit, for reading an ice face by one lamp, and
  cold blood that keeps the hands working and makes the heated upper city sicken them inside a day. The Edict also keeps
  the headcount out of the citizen units, in which every vote is cast.
- **Consequences.** The **Proletarii** are made: *capite censi*, counted by the head. The upper city calls them
  *cuniculi*, rabbits; the miners keep the word for its other meaning, a mine tunnel. The Edict is cut into the wall of
  the Curia and survives the Secession, the Republic and the Greening. Mars's later pitch of citizenship for all has a
  hole in it from the start.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Mars][E-castes]; [endgame/mars.md §B][X-mars].

### 4371 — The Metic vote
- **What happened.** The fuel trade has grown faster than the citizen families can work it, and contract workers from
  Earth, Mars and Jupiter have come to Titan through the 4300s. The Ekklesia votes, by six votes, that anyone who stays
  past one Titan year must take a work edit (denser bone and thick muscle for hauling, blood that holds heat, eyes as
  large as a citizen's, too heavy to fly), and that the edit gives a right to stay and nothing else. A council of five
  hundred drawn by lot writes the terms.
- **Consequences.** The **Metics** (*metoikoi*, "those who live with us") are made. They call themselves *Enchorioi*,
  "the locals", because most have been on Titan longer than the families who say they sprang from it. A Metic needs a
  citizen sponsor, a *prostates*, to sign a lease, sue or marry. Nobody elected the people who decided it, and nobody
  can be blamed for it. It is the last caste edit in the system.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Saturn][E-castes]; [endgame/saturn.md §B][X-saturn] (`meticEdit: 4371`).

## 6. The Sundering (4100–4619)

"Each colony stopped asking the Metropolis for permission at a different time and for a different reason. Some broke
away by vote, some by war, one by simply not answering" ([LORE, "The Sundering"][L-history]). In order: Saturn by
silence (about 4100), Mars by war (4402–4419), Jupiter by a short war (4471), Venus by acclamation (4488), Mercury by
letting its contract run out (4503), the Moon by the Compact of the Sanctuary (4612).

### 4088 — The last archon of Titan
- **What happened.** The League's governor on Titan, the archon, dies in office. The Ekklesia, which has run Titan's
  own business for five centuries, does not ask for a replacement.
- **Consequences.** For twelve years League messages are answered by a clerk of the council.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Earth and the others][L-saturn].

### about 4100 — The Silence
- **What happened.** Titan stops answering. There is no declaration. The council clerk's replies get shorter, then
  stop. Holkas tankers keep carrying fuel inward and the price keeps being paid. An old charter routine in Titan's birth
  clinics is never switched off, and every birth on Titan keeps being sent to Earth's relays for the next nineteen
  centuries.
- **Who.** Nobody in particular, which is the point.
- **Consequences.** Saturn is the first to break away, and the only one that never said so. The Autochthones count
  their Saturn years from the Silence. The birth files on the Clarke Belt Relays are what Saturn hook 2 turns on.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn][L-saturn]; [endgame/saturn.md §C][X-saturn] (`stoppedAnswering: 4100`); Saturn hook 2.

### 4106 — The kleroterion
- **What happened.** With no archon, the Ekklesia fills its offices by lot, from all citizens, each year, using an
  allotment machine named for the one in Athens. Only the ten generals are elected; they command by turns, a day each.
- **Consequences.** The **Ekklesia of Titan** as LORE.md describes it. The council of five hundred, chosen by lot,
  prepares the assembly's business.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Society; Government][L-saturn].

### 4140 — The Decree of the Soil
- **What happened.** The Ekklesia votes that the citizens of Titan were born of Titan and came from nowhere else. The
  word *Autochthones* enters the law.
- **Consequences.** Saturn denies it came from Earth. It also denies it came from Callisto, which matters more later
  (4955). The League debates sending a fleet to an hour-away moon that still sells it fuel, and decides not to.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn: Name; Earth and the others][L-saturn]; [LORE, Earth ("ignored by Saturn")][L-earth].

### 4277 — The Ice Levy
- **What happened.** The League taxes the water Mars sells to the asteroid prospectors and the Moon.
- **Consequences.** The Comitia refuses to collect it. The League's governor collects it with League marines.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Earth and the others][L-mars].

### 4281 — Governor Corvin Sale
- **What happened.** The League appoints Corvin Sale governor of Mars to enforce the Levy. Sale is efficient, unpopular
  and stays.
- **Consequences.** Mars begins to arm. The **Campus Martius** is founded in 4340 on the lava plain below Arsia's flank,
  outside the tubes, as a militia school: the one place Quirites train under the open sky, in suits, in the cold.
  Cadets cross a Corvus bridge under fire before they ever fly a Velite.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §4][E-rival]; [endgame/mars.md §E][X-mars].

### 4380 — Governor for life
- **What happened.** Sale has his term extended by League decree, then extends it himself, without limit.
- **Consequences.** To the Comitia it is a king. Kingship becomes "the oldest Martian crime".
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Values and fears; Culture][L-mars].

### 4402 — The Expulsion and the Republic
- **What happened.** The Comitia expels Sale and the League garrison from Urbs Arsia. It proclaims the **Martian
  Republic**: a Senate of former magistrates, two consuls elected each Mars year, tribunes with a veto, citizens voting
  in their legions, and the rule that no consul may hold office twice in ten years. The Censors' Edict stays on the
  Curia wall.
- **Who.** The first consuls, **Marcus Varro Arsianus** and **Publia Tullia Rufa**.
- **Consequences.** The **Secession War** begins. The Senate meets on the Quirinal, the high gallery of the tubes.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Society; Government][L-mars]; [CONQUEST.md, Mars (the Quirinal)][CONQ].

### 4402–4419 — The Secession War
- **What happened.** Seventeen years of war between Mars and the League.
  - **4404:** the Synedrion votes war by two thirds. **Three member nations vote no and leave the League** with their
    share of the navy, the only nations ever to do so. They come back in 4460, on terms (below).
  - **4406, the Battle of Phobos:** Mars takes the old nursery ring near Phobos and makes it a legionary camp, **Castra
    Phobos**. **Castra Deimos** is built to the same plan in 4408, down to the numbering of the bunks.
  - **4409:** both sides hire Mercury companies. Mercury is still a League colony, and its charter lets its people hire
    out.
  - **4411, the Contract at Tharsis:** two Hemerodrome companies, one under contract to the League and one to Mars,
    meet on the Tharsis plain. Both keep their contracts. Half of each company dies.
  - **4413, the Siege of Urbs Arsia:** League Metropolis carriers fail to take the lava tubes. The first Testudo
    landers carry Martian cohorts into League stations in the counter-attack.
  - **4416:** Martian raiders cut the Clarke Belt Relays for a month.
- **Consequences.** The first Rostrata, Corvus and Velite classes are built in this war, one plan from every yard.
  A legion that loses its standard is disbanded, from the Ninth, at Phobos, onward.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars; Earth ("fought Mars in the Secession and has not forgiven it")][L-mars];
  [FEUDS §3 (Mars ⚔ Earth)][F-feuds].

### 4411 — The law of the contract (Mercury)
- **What happened.** After Tharsis the ephors write into law what the companies did: a contract is kept even against
  one's own people, and a broken contract shames the whole company. A shield is brought home or its bearer is not.
- **Consequences.** **Mercury becomes the mercenary house.** It hires out to anyone who pays and is never in a blood
  feud. The sealed contract, both halves of a message staff carried by Skytale, dates from the same law. Mercury hook 2
  is Tharsis come round again.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: Government; Culture][L-mercury]; [FEUDS §3 (Mercury)][F-feuds];
  [endgame/mercury.md §A][X-mercury].

### 4419 — The Treaty of Phobos
- **What happened.** The war ends in a treaty signed at Castra Phobos. The League recognises the Republic's government
  in fact; Mars keeps its two moons; and the treaty fixes Mars's borders as Mars, Phobos and Deimos, "and nothing
  further".
- **Consequences.** Earth has not forgiven the Secession. Mars still says it is the true Rome and Earth the decayed
  empire. Earth's officers still quote the border clause at any Mars commander who takes another world.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3][F-feuds]; [endgame/earth.md §E][X-earth] ("By the treaty that ended the Secession, this
  world was never yours").

### 4420 — Sale at Kallistopolis
- **What happened.** Corvin Sale, the deposed governor, flees outward and is received at the court of the House of
  Callisto as an honoured guest. He dies there in 4431 with a Companion's rank.
- **Consequences.** To Mars, the Jovian kings are the people who shelter kings. The hatred of a republic for the crowns
  starts here, a generation before the first belt claim.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars ("a republic against kings, as Rome fought Macedon")][L-mars]; [FEUDS §3][F-feuds].

### 4423 — The Strategeion
- **What happened.** The League, having lost a war to colonists it did not understand, founds an officer school in the
  spin ring at Pharos Station. Cadets from every member nation ride the tether up on their first day and do not come
  down until they graduate. They study treaty law and the rules of engagement before they touch a gun, train in rings
  spun to every colony's gravity with weights on their wrists, and pass a final test in a mixed-flag squadron where any
  captain may refuse an order.
- **Consequences.** Graduates wear brass weight cuffs for life, so their bones stay Earth's. Contract hands cook for the
  cadets and fly their target drones.
- **Supports.** [endgame/earth.md §E][X-earth].

### 4430 — The Greening is begun
- **What happened.** The Senate votes to make Mars green. Aqua Borealis is rebuilt to carry ten times the water. The
  engineers say it will take a thousand years.
- **Consequences.** The Greening becomes the Republic's civic faith. The names of those who did the work are kept on the
  **Greening rolls**: engineers and consuls, never miners. The estimate has been "another thousand years" ever since.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Society; Culture][L-mars]; [endgame/mars.md §B][X-mars].

### 4431 — The first Triumph
- **What happened.** The consul who held Phobos rides through Urbs Arsia with a slave at the consul's ear repeating
  that the consul is mortal.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Culture][L-mars].

### 4460 — The Readmission
- **What happened.** The three nations that left the League in 4404 ask to come back. The Synedrion readmits them at a
  price: a smaller share of the navy and no Strategos from among them for a century.
- **Consequences.** The only time anyone has rejoined the League. Its terms are the precedent the Synedrion argues over
  in Earth hook 2.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Government ("none has since the Secession")][L-earth]; Earth hooks 1 and 2.

### 4471 — The Short War at Europa
- **What happened.** The League tries to put a garrison back into the Third Crown station at Europa to collect a
  tribute the crowns stopped paying during the Secession. Callisto, Ganymede and Europa act together for once and
  drive it out in a nine-day war.
- **Consequences.** The League, two years out of one war, will not fight another so far away. In 4476 it recognises
  the three crowns as sovereign. Callisto offers a royal marriage to the ruling family of a League nation to mark the
  peace; the Synedrion declines. The Diadochoi still respect Earth more than anyone does, "as one dynasty respects an
  older one", and still want to marry into it.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Earth and the others][L-jupiter]; [LORE, Earth ("flattered")][L-earth].

### 4488 — The Throne stops asking
- **What happened.** Autokrator **Michael Doukaites** is acclaimed in the Hippodrome and does not send to Quito for
  confirmation. Nobody in the Synedrion moves to make him.
- **Consequences.** Venus breaks away by acclamation, which on Venus is a vote. The Nephelai call themselves Earth's
  true heirs from here: the people whose bodies changed least, and who did not need a war to leave. Earth finds them
  charming and does not trust them.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Earth and the others][L-venus].

### 4503 — The Contract of the Rail runs out
- **What happened.** Mercury's colonial charter was written as a term contract to run the rail for three hundred
  Mercury years, renewable. It reaches its term. The ephors vote not to renew.
- **Consequences.** Mercury breaks away without breaking anything: it lets a contract run out, which is how it has
  treated every contract since. The diarchy, already three centuries old, is now sovereign. Mercury sees Earth as soft
  from this year on, because Earth let it go.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury][L-mercury]; [endgame/mercury.md §A][X-mercury] ("Mercury never breaks a contract. It
  lets one run out.").

### 4561 — The Withdrawal to the Canyon (Mars)
- **What happened.** The headcount, told in 4402 that the Republic would free them, are still counted and still
  voteless. Eleven thousand miners walk out of the shafts and down the floor of Valles Marineris. The Senate sends no
  legion; it sends a tribune with a promise of the vote within a generation.
- **Consequences.** The miners go back. The promise is not kept. The road they walked becomes the **Via Marineris**,
  with a fort at each day's march, built in the next decade to make sure they cannot walk it again.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Bases (Via Marineris)][L-mars]; [ENDGAME §2, Mars][E-castes].

### 4598 — The Lunar Schedule question
- **What happened.** With every other colony gone, a League jurist argues that the Charter's Lunar Schedule (3000)
  still makes the Moon League territory. The Synedrion debates it for fourteen years.
- **Consequences.** It ends in the Compact of the Sanctuary, which leaves the Schedule unrepealed.
- **Supports.** Moon hook 3 ([LORE, Moon: Plot hooks][L-moon]).

### 4612 — The Compact of the Sanctuary
- **What happened.** The Moon's settler assembly hands its government to the College of the Pythia and declares the
  Moon a sanctuary, belonging to no nation. The Amphictyony is remade with one seat for each of the seven houses, and
  takes on the duty of declaring a **Sacred War** against anyone who attacks the sanctuary. Earth accepts because it
  keeps its seat, its treasury and the closest ties. Nobody repeals the Lunar Schedule.
- **Consequences.** The Moon breaks away last, by vote, and without leaving Earth's side. The Selenitai almost never
  fight for themselves from here on. The Sanctuary of the Pythia, as LORE.md describes it, dates from this year.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Society; Government][L-moon]; [FEUDS §3 (the Moon)][F-feuds]; Moon hook 3.

### 4615 — The Theoros oath
- **What happened.** Every house swears to let the Pythia's envoy ship, the Theoros, pass. It is small, unarmed, and
  carries a laurel ring at the bow.
- **Consequences.** The oath has been broken twice: by Venus in the First Sacred War (4793) and by Mars in 5329. Both
  times the Amphictyony called a Sacred War.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Ship classes (Theoros)][L-moon].

### 4619 — The first Amphictyony of Seven
- **What happened.** Seven seats sit in the Hall of Answers under the fixed Earth for the first time: Earth, the Moon,
  Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. Saturn's seat is filled by a citizen drawn by lot, who flies in, votes, and
  goes home.
- **Consequences.** "Today seven nations hold the solar system." The Sundering is over.
- **Supports.** [LORE, "The Sundering"][L-history].

## 7. The Age of the Seven (4620–5949)

Thirteen centuries of seven houses. The feuds in [FEUDS.md][F-feuds] all begin or harden here: the belt wars, the Nika
Burning, the cousins claim, the ice embargo, the quarrel of the two crowns. The god-armour is built over every world.
The low castes rise, and are put down.

### 4630 — Tholos Station
- **What happened.** The Amphictyony builds a station in lunar orbit: a ring of white columns round a domed core, open
  to the stars, with a silver dish on top.
- **Consequences.** It is the sanctuary's front door and the target of the first Sacred War.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Bases][L-moon].

### 4640 — The Phylakterion
- **What happened.** The Amphictyony founds a school on Tholos Station, its guard-house, to train officers who will
  command other people's fleets in a Sacred War. Cadets come from every house; Selenites earn a place by the College's
  examination. Each learns to fly all seven cruisers, rides escort on the Theoros, and spends a year reading treaty law.
  The final test is to command a borrowed fleet through a sham Sacred War without firing first.
- **Consequences.** Graduates take the title *Hieromnemon*, "sacred remembrancer". The Phylakterion is the rival's
  school when the Moon is the player's blood enemy.
- **Supports.** [endgame/moon.md §E][X-moon].

### 4655 — The lamps go unanswered
- **What happened.** On Founding Day the Moon, the last colony still lighting a lamp back to Earth, does not.
- **Consequences.** "The colonies do not light one back." No colony has since. The three-thousandth Founding Day, in
  the game's year, asks the player whether to be the first.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Culture][L-earth]; [endgame/00-system.md, SYS-11][X-system].

### 4690–4732 — The prospectors' belt
- **What happened.** Martian and Jovian prospectors work the asteroid belt, which lies between them. Claims overlap.
  Mars says the belt is a frontier of the Republic; the crowns say it is the road to their door.
- **Consequences.** "They want the asteroid belt ... and so does everyone else." Mars and Jupiter both build stations
  on Ceres.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Earth and the others][L-mars].

### 4733–4741 — The First Belt War
- **What happened.** A Martian legion boards a Callisto ore ship off Ceres. Callisto and Ganymede, for once on the same
  side, answer with their fleets. The decisive fight is the **Battle of Ceres** in 4736: Corvus boarding cruisers
  against Sarissa spinal-gun cruisers, first used in this war. Mars holds the inner belt; the crowns hold the outer.
- **Who.** Consul **Servius Aelius Calvus**; the kings of Callisto and Ganymede, flying together.
- **Consequences.** **Mars ⚔ Jupiter becomes a blood feud.** The pattern for the next twelve centuries is set: the two
  Jovian houses fight each other more than anyone else and stop to fight Mars together. The war ends in the Truce of
  Ceres, which neither side calls a peace.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Government][L-jupiter]; [FEUDS §3 (Mars ⚔ Jupiter)][F-feuds].

### 4791 — The Nika Burning
- **What happened.** Selenite factors in Nephelokokkygia finance and run the **Blue** faction; the court families of
  the capital run the **Greens**. A disputed race in the Hippodrome becomes a riot under the old cry "*Nika!*",
  "Conquer!". The Greens burn the Selenite counting-houses. Blue crews cut lift cells in reply. In the war that follows
  two cloud cities, **Aerope** and **Kallinike**, lose lift and sink into the heat with nearly everyone aboard.
- **Who.** The Selenite factors of the Blues; the Green families; Autokrator **Theodora Akropolitissa**, who orders the
  Selenites out of every Venus city.
- **Consequences.** **Moon ⚔ Venus becomes a blood feud.** The Blues are remade as a purely Venusian faction, the
  great families of Chrysopolis. No race between the Greens and the Blues has been run since (Venus hook 3).
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Earth and the others][L-venus]; [FEUDS §3 (Moon ⚔ Venus)][F-feuds]; Venus hook 3.

### 4792 — The first Siphon
- **What happened.** Venus builds fire ships against Selenite-hired Mercury companies who board its cities: gilded tubes
  with beast-headed nozzles that pour burning plasma at close range.
- **Consequences.** "Venus's answer to boarders." The Greek Fire Works in the cloud layer date from this year.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Ship classes (Siphon)][L-venus]; [CONQUEST.md, Venus][CONQ].

### 4793–4796 — The First Sacred War
- **What happened.** Venusian Siphons attack Tholos Station and stop a Theoros, breaking the oath of 4615. The
  Amphictyony declares a Sacred War. Earth, Mars and Jupiter send fleets; the Moon pays Mercury companies. Venus is
  blockaded for three years, pays an indemnity from its treasury under Pytho, and keeps the expulsion of the Selenites.
- **Consequences.** The Sacred War rule works, once. Every house learns that attacking the Moon brings everyone Friendly
  with it down on the attacker ([FEUDS §3][F-feuds]). Venus learns to pay, marry or bribe before it fights.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Government][L-moon]; [LORE, Venus: Government][L-venus].

### 4797 — The Varangian Station
- **What happened.** The Autokrator hires a standing Hemerodrome guard and houses it in a fortified station in the
  cloud layer.
- **Consequences.** Venus's palace has been guarded by foreign soldiers ever since; it is the first standing contract
  Mercury signs with any house. Mercury despises Venus and has guarded its throne for twelve centuries.
- **Supports.** [CONQUEST.md, Venus (the Varangian Station)][CONQ]; [LORE, Venus: Government][L-venus].

### 4810 — The Magnaura
- **What happened.** After the Sacred War, Venus founds an officer school in a domed hall under the Sacred Palace.
  Cadets are court children only; envelope-born are refused. They fly Smaragdos seven laps round the outer course
  between the city's tethers, as in the old chariot races. A cadet who touches a tether is sent home; one who touches
  the envelope is sent down.
- **Consequences.** Graduates take a court title and a family's paint. The Magnaura is the rival's school when Venus is
  the player's blood enemy.
- **Supports.** [endgame/venus.md §E][X-venus].

### 4861–4874 — The Second Belt War
- **What happened.** Mars takes Vesta. The crowns take it back. Thirteen years, two dictators named by the Senate,
  both of whom lay down their power at six months.
- **Consequences.** The first Tessarakonteres lost in battle, Ganymede's *Andragathia*, at Hygiea in 4870. Its
  king's name is chiselled off the walls for leaving it.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Government (dictator)][L-mars]; [LORE, Jupiter: Culture][L-jupiter].

### 4911–4930 — The War of the Europan Succession
- **What happened.** The last king of Europa dies without children. Callisto and Ganymede both claim the moon through
  marriages. They fight for nineteen years.
- **Consequences.** Europa is shared: the **Third Crown** station off Europa is held by both houses at peace and fought
  over at war. "Two kingdoms remain strong." Royal weddings between the houses have ended four wars since, and started
  three.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Society; Bases (Third Crown)][L-jupiter].

### 4955 — The Kinship Rescript
- **What happened.** The King of Callisto issues a rescript naming the people of Titan "our cousins", citing the
  Jovian crews of the Titan expedition (3478), and offering them the protection of the crown.
- **Consequences.** The Ekklesia answers with a vote to sell Callisto no fuel for a year. **Jupiter → Saturn becomes a
  claim feud.** It has been offered again by nearly every king of Callisto since, and refused every time.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Saturn ("our cousins"); Jupiter ("quietly think Saturn belongs to them")][L-saturn];
  [FEUDS §3][F-feuds]; Saturn hook 3.

### 5000 — The two-thousandth Founding Day
- **What happened.** Earth lights its lamps and invites every house to Quito. The Moon sends the Pythia's envoy. Nobody
  else comes.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Earth: Culture][L-earth].

### 5030–5210 — The Vault of Gaia
- **What happened.** Earth builds the first god-armour: a lattice shell over the whole globe, a net of struts with
  large open windows between them, garden terraces in green and gold in some windows, solid belts crossing the poles.
  It is built for Gaia, Earth herself, the mother of all.
- **Consequences.** The open windows keep Earth's cities "the only ones in the system under an open sky". Every other
  house reads it as a fortress and starts its own.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Earth][GA]; [endgame/earth.md §F][X-earth].

### 5140–5162 — The Third Belt War and the Ceres Partition
- **What happened.** The longest belt war. It ends with Ceres divided along a line through its main crater: the inner
  docks to Mars, the outer to the crowns.
- **Consequences.** The line runs through a station corridor, with a guard on each side. It is the front of every belt
  war since.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3][F-feuds].

### 5170–5260 — The Ancile
- **What happened.** Mars builds a figure-eight shield over its Earthward face, of dagger-wedge plates laid in chevrons
  with twelve bosses. It is named for the shield of Mars that fell from the sky in Roman legend and was kept by his
  priests.
- **Consequences.** It faces Earth, which every Quirite understands.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Mars][GA].

### 5240–5390 — The Talons of Zeus
- **What happened.** The two crowns, at peace after a royal wedding, build god-armour round Jupiter itself: great talons
  closing on the planet, a zigzag channel of light, the thunderbolt, down each. Each house builds half, and each says
  its half is taller.
- **Consequences.** The largest structure in the system. It is round Jupiter and not round Callisto, which says what the
  crowns think they own.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Jupiter][GA].

### 5300–5340 — Selene's Veil
- **What happened.** The Amphictyony votes to pay for a crescent of plating over the Moon's Earthward face, its horns
  round the limb and a veil-band arching over the pole, from the interest on the treasuries.
- **Consequences.** Every house paid for the armour over the Moon, which is the Moon's idea of neutrality.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Moon][GA].

### 5310 — The Walk-Off at Lagrange (Earth)
- **What happened.** Contract hands at Lagrange Yard stop work over a contract term extended without pay. The Strategos
  hires a Hemerodrome company to restore the yard. Half the strikers are Hemerodrome helots; the company keeps its
  contract against them.
- **Consequences.** The yard runs again inside a month. The hands learn that Mercury's law is kept even against
  Mercury's own, and that Earth will pay for it.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Earth][E-castes]; [endgame/earth.md §B][X-earth].

### 5329 — The Second Sacred War
- **What happened.** A Martian consul seizes a Theoros carrying a Pythia's answer to Jupiter, to read it. The
  Amphictyony declares a Sacred War on Mars. Jupiter and Venus fight it gladly; Earth sends ships late.
- **Consequences.** The Republic gives the envoy back unopened and pays. The consul is tried and exiled. The Pythia's
  answers to Mars are sold at a higher fee from then on.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Government][L-moon]; Moon hook 1.

### 5350–5420 — The Shell of Aphrodite
- **What happened.** Venus builds a scallop shell over its Earthward hemisphere, fluted ribs fanning from a hinge, the
  cloud deck cupped inside it like a pearl. It is paid for by the great families, each rib by one family.
- **Consequences.** A family that loses favour loses its rib's maintenance contract with its lift contract.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Venus][GA].

### 5402–5419 — The Oberon Ark
- **What happened.** Earth announces the first new colony in 1,812 years, on Oberon, a moon of Uranus, and sends an Ark
  from Lagrange Yard. It reaches Oberon in 5409. Its messages stop in 5419. A Pathfinder sent after it finds the ring
  stopped and nobody aboard.
- **Consequences.** No colony has been announced since. An Ark has stood half-built at Lagrange Yard most decades
  anyway, because the yard keeps the trade alive. That is why nobody asks questions about the one in Dry Dock One.
- **Supports.** Earth hook 3 ([LORE, Earth: Plot hooks][L-earth]).

### 5410–5470 — The Kerykeion
- **What happened.** Mercury builds a staff of two snakes and a pair of wings round its spin axis, for Hermes, the
  herald. Markets sit where the snakes cross. Mercury pays for it in power, sold forward to Venus for sixty years.
- **Consequences.** Mercury's god is Hermes and its people are Spartan; nobody on Mercury sees a problem with that.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Mercury][GA].

### 5480–5560 — The Harpe
- **What happened.** The Ekklesia votes, by four hands, to build god-armour: a sickle under Saturn's rings, for
  Kronos, whose temple in Rome held the treasury.
- **Consequences.** The last god-armour. Every world now carries one; every Katabasis in the game has to crack one.
- **Supports.** [GOD-ARMOUR.md, Saturn][GA]; [ENDGAME §3 (World 6)][E-katabasis].

### 5512–5530 — The Fourth Belt War
- **What happened.** Fought mostly by hired Mercury companies on both sides. At the **Battle of Pallas** in 5521 a
  company under contract to Mars and a company under contract to Ganymede fight to the last ship.
- **Consequences.** It ends in the Treaty of Vesta, which redraws the Ceres line by one corridor.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3][F-feuds]; Mercury hook 2.

### 5544 — The Second Io Rising
- **What happened.** The Laoi take the lift head and hold the Io Prison-fort for forty days. Both crowns, at war with
  each other, call a truce to retake it. The Royal Pages fly as the kings' wing.
- **Consequences.** Every gang now gives a hostage child to the palaces; before 5544 only every second gang did. Moiro's
  grandson is one of the 5990s hostages.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Jupiter][E-castes]; [endgame/jupiter.md §B][X-jupiter].

### 5603 — The Ice Embargo
- **What happened.** The Greening needs more water than Mars has. The Senate asks to buy ring ice from Stoa Station in
  bulk, for centuries. The Ekklesia votes no by eleven hands: selling Mars the rings would make Mars the owner of the
  rings.
- **Consequences.** **Mars → Saturn becomes a claim feud.** Mars's terraforming needs the ice of Saturn's rings, and
  Saturn says no (Mars hook 2).
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3][F-feuds]; [LORE, Mars: Plot hooks][L-mars].

### 5640 — The Metic Petition (Saturn)
- **What happened.** Eleven thousand Metics sign a petition to be entered on the deme rolls. The Ekklesia rejects it by
  one vote. The Metic crews at Stoa Station stop the skimmers for nine days; the council of five hundred, drawn by lot,
  suspends the Metic right to stay for anyone who struck.
- **Consequences.** The strike ends. The citizens learn that the vote and the fuel can both be taken from them by people
  who have neither.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Saturn][E-castes]; [endgame/saturn.md §B][X-saturn].

### 5688 — The Diadem Affront
- **What happened.** At a peace conference on Pharos Station, the Autokrator's envoy and the King of Callisto's both
  demand to be seated first, as the elder crown. The conference breaks up.
- **Consequences.** **Venus → Jupiter becomes a claim feud**: two crowns that each think they are the real one.
  Venus has paid Mercury to raid Jovian shipping in every belt war since.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3][F-feuds]; [LORE, "Who stands where" (gaudy rivals)][L-stands].

### 5702 — The examination rule (Moon)
- **What happened.** The College rules that no one in debt may sit its examination.
- **Consequences.** No Hierodoulos can become a scholar. The *Seisachtheia*, a shaking-off of the debt as Solon gave
  Athens, becomes what the keepers want before anything else.
- **Supports.** [endgame/moon.md §B][X-moon].

### 5762 — The Long Dictatorship that wasn't
- **What happened.** In a belt war crisis the Senate names a dictator, **Appius Sergius Fidenas**, for six months. At
  the end of the six months Fidenas has not laid down the office. On the ninth day over, a tribune vetoes the army's
  pay and the legions stand down; Fidenas lays it down and is exiled.
- **Consequences.** Mars has named dictators since, and feared every one.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Government; Values and fears][L-mars].

### 5891 — The Arrears of Chrysopolis
- **What happened.** A great Blue family of Chrysopolis stops paying for its private vault under Pytho. The Amphictyony
  debates opening it for a year, then the family pays.
- **Consequences.** It sets the procedure LORE.md's Moon hook 2 follows: a year unpaid, then a vote of the Amphictyony
  on opening the vault. Nobody has ever reached the vote.
- **Supports.** Moon hook 2; [endgame/moon.md §C][X-moon] ("A treasury under Pytho has gone a year unpaid").

### 5928–5939 — The Yard Committee
- **What happened.** A closed committee of the Synedrion decides, across twelve Strategoi, to do what Earth swore in
  3131 never to do: make edited children of its own. They are to work free fall like a Moon hand and stand up at a full
  1 g like a Human: short, thick-boned, broad in the hand. No colony ever made that body.
- **Who.** The Yard Committee. Each Strategos was told in the same week as the Contract of Pharos, and each signed.
- **Consequences.** The Contract of Pharos had made Earth depend on edited people for 2,700 years. The Committee decides
  Earth should own some.
- **Supports.** [endgame/earth.md §B][X-earth] (`arkStrategoiCount: 12`).

### 5940 — The Ark in Dry Dock One
- **What happened.** An Ark is laid down at Lagrange Yard "for an unannounced colony". Its ring is welded by a
  Hemerodrome company under weld boss Aster, who never asks what for. Within a few years its ring is turning at a full
  g with Earth's first edited children inside. The yard paperwork calls them *Ark crew*.
- **Consequences.** Earth hook 3. The Selenite yard clerk Melite bills the Ark crew's hours to contracts that ended
  long ago, and is still doing it in 6000.
- **Supports.** [ENDGAME §2, Earth][E-castes]; [endgame/earth.md §B, §C][X-earth] (`arkStartYear: 5940`).

## 8. The last fifty years (5950–5999)

Every crisis hook in LORE.md is loaded in these fifty years and none of them has fired. The hooks fire in play, as
cards ([FEUDS §5][FEUDS], [ENDGAME §2][E-castes]); this section ends where the game begins. Names of sitting rulers are
invented here unless a house file gives them.

### 5951 — Demetrios XI of Ganymede
- **What happened.** Demetrios Kastellos XI is acclaimed by the army of Ganymede over his elder sister, who had been
  named heir. She is sent to the Third Crown station and dies there in 5958.
- **Consequences.** Ganymede's king has one child, a daughter, **Phila Kastellissa**, born 5979. She is the bride in
  Jupiter hook 1.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Government ("whoever the army acclaims")][L-jupiter].

### 5953 — The Cold Wells failure
- **What happened.** One of the three ice craters at Mercury's north pole collapses. Lakedaimon goes on short water for
  two circuits. The helots, who need more water than citizens in the heat, get least.
- **Consequences.** The forward gang starts keeping its own water stores. Its foreman in 6000 is **Aristomene**, who
  learned the trade in those two circuits.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: Bases (the Cold Wells)][L-mercury]; [endgame/mercury.md §B, MER-E5][X-mercury].

### 5956 — Pythia Kleobule
- **What happened.** The College elects **Kleobule of the Long Hall** Pythia, for life. She is the first Pythia who
  was ever a copyist in the copy-hall.
- **Consequences.** She sells an answer to every side of every war, as her predecessors did, and raises the fee for
  each one. She is the Pythia when the game opens.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Moon: Government][L-moon]; [endgame/moon.md §A][X-moon].

### 5958 — The Pallas skirmish
- **What happened.** A Martian patrol and a Callisto squadron fire on each other at Pallas during the Truce of Vesta.
  Both sides say the other fired first. The Pythia sells both sides the same answer about who did.
- **Consequences.** The Truce of Vesta is dead in all but name.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3 (Mars ⚔ Jupiter)][F-feuds].

### 5961 — The Fifth Belt War begins
- **What happened.** Callisto's ships take the outer docks of the Ceres line and the corridor Mars won in 5530. The
  Senate votes war. Ganymede joins Callisto after a year.
- **Consequences.** **This is the war "already being fought when the game starts"** ([FEUDS §3][F-feuds]). It has run
  thirty-nine years by 6000, with three truces, none lasting a Mars year. The front is the Ceres line.
- **Supports.** [FEUDS §3, §5][F-feuds]; [endgame/mars.md §A][X-mars].

### 5964 — Dictator for six months
- **What happened.** After the loss of Castra Vesta, the Senate names **Titus Pomponius Calenus** dictator. He lays the
  office down at six months to the day.
- **Consequences.** It is the last time Mars trusts one person with the whole war. The Senate spreads command across
  many legates instead, one of whom is Asper.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mars: Government][L-mars].

### 5966 — Autokrator Konstantina
- **What happened.** **Konstantina Akropolitissa**, of the line of the Autokrator who expelled the Selenites in 4791,
  is acclaimed in the Hippodrome. She is sixty-one. She names no co-emperor.
- **Consequences.** By 6000 she is ninety-five and still names none. Her chamberlain, the *parakoimomenos*, runs the
  palace. The Blue families of Chrysopolis have a general of their own. The sent-down at Maxwell Anchor have a miner
  they listen to. These are the three crowns of Venus hook 1.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Venus: Government][L-venus]; Venus hook 1; [endgame/venus.md, VEN-02][X-venus].

### 5968 — Charillos, King of the Rail
- **What happened.** **Charillos** succeeds his mother as King of the Rail, having finished the agoge and the Dawn Run.
- **Consequences.** He is the king in Mercury hook 3. **Eudamia**, King of the Field since 5959, leads the army in
  person and is out with a company most of every year.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Mercury: Government][L-mercury].

### 5971 — Arkas XIX of Callisto
- **What happened.** **Arkas XIX** is acclaimed King of Callisto after his brother dies in the belt war.
- **Consequences.** He is the king in Jupiter hooks 1 and 3: the bridegroom's father and the man who wants Earth to
  call him king of all Jupiter. His heir, Prince **Danel**, was a Royal Page.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Plot hooks][L-jupiter].

### 5974 — The Chrysopolis contract
- **What happened.** The lift contract for Chrysopolis passes to a Blue family, the Argyroi, as payment for a loan to
  the throne. Its Paroikoi pass with it, under **Marina of the North Seam**, warden of the crown.
- **Consequences.** The Argyroi cut the crown crews' rations to pay the loan back. Marina's crews start keeping their
  own accounts.
- **Supports.** [endgame/venus.md §B, §C][X-venus].

### 5975 — Lykon is born
- **What happened.** **Lykon** is born in the Io Sulphur Pits to a deep gang. **Moiro** is already its shift-mother.
- **Supports.** [endgame/jupiter.md §B][X-jupiter].

### 5978 — Founders' Day at Kallistopolis
- **What happened.** The four crowns are carried through the ice palaces, the hostage children walking behind. A
  hostage boy from Io drops the crown of Io and is beaten for it.
- **Consequences.** The story of the "fifth crown" is told again in the pits that year.
- **Supports.** [LORE, Jupiter: Culture][L-jupiter]; [endgame/jupiter.md, JUP-E3][X-jupiter].

### 5979 — The Strategeion's last full class
- **What happened.** The Strategeion graduates its largest class since the Secession. Its top cadet is the Navarch the
  player meets as Mars's rival (name rolled each game).

## 13. Contradictions and gaps found

**Between the endgame files and the sources, and how this file reconciled them.**

1. **earth.md: twelve Strategoi for an Ark laid down in 5940.** A Strategos serves one year and may not serve twice
   running, so 5940 to 6000 is about sixty Strategoi, not twelve. Reconciled: the **Yard Committee decided it across the
   twelve Strategoi of 5928–5939**, and every Strategos since has been told. earth.md's wording "decided it across twelve
   Strategoi" fits this; `arkStrategoiCount: 12` is the count of deciders, not of years since.
2. **earth.md: the Contract of Pharos (3200) uses the Moon's bonded "sold on by the treasuries"**, but moon.md digs the
   Treasuries about 3400. Reconciled: in 3200 the Moon hands are bound by passage debt; from 3404 they are debtors sold
   on by the Treasuries.
3. **earth.md: the Contract of Pharos (3200) uses Mercury's helots.** LORE.md settles Mercury after Venus. Reconciled by
   dating Venus 3168/3184 and Lakedaimon 3192, with the helot edit in its founding year (mercury.md), before 3200.
4. **venus.md: the first Autokrator signs the Novel of the Envelope "in the first generation of the cloud cities"**, yet
   LORE.md puts Venus's break from Earth in the Sundering. Reconciled: the title is a League-tolerated style from 3203;
